## What "best" means depends on why you are asking

Four people ask this question in a week, and they want four different answers.

- **The traveller with one free morning.** You want a class you can walk into today, without booking, where nobody minds that you have never done yoga.
- **The beginner who is nervous.** You want a small group, a teacher who watches you, and permission to stop.
- **The experienced practitioner.** You want depth: real pranayama, held postures, and someone who can tell you something you did not already know.
- **The person thinking about teaching.** You want to sit in a room and work out whether you would want to spend twenty-eight days with this teacher.

A class built for the first person is rarely right for the third. That is not a failing; it is a different room.

## What you will actually find in Fort Kochi

Fort Kochi is small, and the yoga in it falls into a few broad kinds.

- **Small independent schools** with daily classes, usually taught by the person who owns them.
- **Hotel and homestay sessions** arranged for guests, often an hour, often private.
- **One-off sessions booked through travel platforms**, priced for holidaymakers and usually booked days ahead.
- **Teacher training schools**, which also run daily classes between courses.

All four can be good. They are not interchangeable. A platform-booked sunrise session on a rooftop is a lovely thing to do once; it is not where you learn to breathe properly.

## Six things worth judging

These are the questions I would ask if I were choosing a class in a town I did not know.

1. **How long is the class?** A traditional Hatha class needs time: postures held rather than rushed, rest between them, breathing at both ends. Ours run two hours. Sixty minutes is a different practice, not a shorter version of the same one.
2. **How many people are in the room?** Nobody can correct thirty bodies. Small groups are the single thing that makes hands-on teaching possible.
3. **Does the teacher correct by hand?** Being adjusted physically — with your permission — teaches you more in one class than a month of copying someone at the front.
4. **Does anyone ask about injuries?** A teacher who does not ask cannot adapt anything for you.
5. **Who teaches it?** In small schools, ask whether the person named on the website is the person in the room.
6. **Can you come once?** If a school will only sell you a package before you have tried a class, you are taking a bet.

## What to ask before you go anywhere

Message any school with these four questions. The answers, and how quickly they come, tell you most of what you need.

- How long is the class, and how many people usually come?
- Who is teaching this week?
- I have *[this injury or limitation]* — can I still join?
- Can I come to one class before deciding anything?

## Common questions

### Which is the best yoga class in Fort Kochi?

There is no single best class. The right one depends on what you want: a walk-in class if you are passing through, a small traditional class if you want to be corrected by hand, or a teacher training if you want to learn to teach. Judge a class on length, group size, whether the teacher adjusts students physically, and whether anyone asks about injuries before you begin.

### Can I join a yoga class in Fort Kochi without booking?

At our school, yes. Classes run every day at 7:30–9:30 am and 3:30–5:30 pm, cost ₹700 a class, and need no booking. Many schools aimed at visitors do ask you to book ahead, so message before you walk over.

### How much does a yoga class in Fort Kochi cost?

Our two-hour drop-in class is ₹700, and a private session is ₹2,000. Sessions booked through travel platforms usually cost several times more.

### Is yoga in Fort Kochi suitable for complete beginners?

Yes. Beginners are welcome in every class we teach, and so are stiff bodies, older bodies and people returning after injury. Tell the teacher before you start and the practice adapts around you.

### What is the difference between a yoga studio and a yoga school?

A studio usually sells classes by the hour, often several styles taught by a rota of teachers. A school teaches one tradition in depth and keeps the same teacher in front of you: here that means Hatha Yoga with pranayama, meditation and Vedanta philosophy, and a teacher training for students who want to go further. Neither is better in the abstract — a studio suits someone collecting a workout, a school suits someone learning a practice.

### Do classes in Fort Kochi include pranayama, or only postures?

Ask, because it varies. Shorter studio classes are often postures alone. Our two-hour class always includes pranayama and rest between postures, which is part of why it runs long — the breathing practice is not an add-on at the end.

### Should I book a yoga retreat instead of joining daily classes?

If you are in Fort Kochi anyway, daily drop-in classes cost far less and let you judge the teaching before committing. A retreat makes sense when you want accommodation, food and practice arranged as one week — ours run through our retreat project, [Samya Retreats](https://samyaretreats.com/), in Fort Kochi and Munnar.
