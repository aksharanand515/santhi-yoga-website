On the mat

Wrist pain in yoga: causes and fixes

The wrist was not designed to carry your body weight at ninety degrees. It can learn to, but only if the hand underneath it is doing its job.

By Master Aksharanand A S, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 27 September 2026 5 min read

In short

Most yoga wrist pain comes from dumping weight into the heel of the hand with the fingers passive. Spreading the fingers, pressing through the knuckles and the base of the index finger, and lifting the centre of the palm spreads the load. Where that is not enough, fists, forearms or a wedge take the angle out.

Wrist pain is the most common complaint in any class with sun salutations in it, and the most reliably fixable. In most cases it is not an injury. It is a loading problem.

Why wrists hurt

In downward dog, plank or chaturanga the wrist sits at roughly ninety degrees with body weight above it. If the weight lands on the heel of the hand — the part nearest the wrist — the small bones at the back of the joint are squeezed together and the front is pulled long. Hold that for a few rounds and it aches.

The hand is meant to share this. Twenty-seven bones, a broad set of knuckles and five fingers exist precisely so the load does not all arrive at one point.

How to use the hand

  • Spread the fingers wide, with the middle finger pointing straight forward.
  • Press through the knuckles, particularly the mound at the base of the index finger and thumb — this is where most people lose the connection and let weight roll back and outward.
  • Grip lightly with the fingertips, as though the mat could be drawn towards you.
  • Lift the centre of the palm a little, so the hand forms a shallow dome rather than a flat slab.

Do that in tabletop first, where there is less weight to manage, and you will feel the difference immediately: the ache moves out of the wrist and into the forearm, which is exactly where it should be.

Two more things. In downward dog, send weight back towards the hips rather than hanging forward over the hands. And turn the hands very slightly outwards if your elbows are hypermobile.

Modifications that work

When technique is not enough, change the angle or take it away.

Fists. Make loose fists and take weight on the flat of the knuckles, keeping the wrist straight. Works in plank and tabletop; harder in downward dog.

Forearms. Dolphin instead of downward dog, forearm plank instead of plank. This removes the wrist from the question entirely and is a genuinely good practice in its own right.

A wedge. Roll the front edge of your mat or use a folded blanket under the heel of the hand, which reduces the angle at the joint by ten or fifteen degrees. Small change, large relief.

Fingertips. For short holds, coming onto the fingertips takes the wrist out of extension altogether. Demanding, so use it sparingly.

Building tolerance

If your wrists hurt only because they are unaccustomed to weight, the answer is not avoidance. It is small, frequent doses.

Thirty seconds in tabletop with good hand technique, rocking gently forward and back over the hands, done most days, changes things within a month. So does simply doing fewer rounds of Surya Namaskar with better hands rather than more rounds with poor ones.

Warming up the wrists

A minute is enough. Circle the wrists in both directions. Press the palms together in front of the chest and lower the hands while keeping the palms joined. Then turn the hands so the fingers point back towards the knees in tabletop and rock gently — this stretches the forearm flexors, which are usually the tight ones in anyone who types.

This matters more for desk workers than for anyone else, and the same forearms are often involved in neck and shoulder pain at a desk.

When it is not the technique

Numbness, tingling or pins and needles in the fingers is a nerve symptom, not a loading problem, and belongs with a doctor. So does pain that persists between sessions, swelling, or a wrist that hurts when you are not using it.

Previous fracture, arthritis or carpal tunnel syndrome do not rule out practice, but they do mean the modifications above should be your normal practice rather than your fallback. Tell your teacher on the first day — in a small class that information actually gets used.

Common questions

Why do my wrists hurt in downward dog?

Usually because weight is falling into the heel of the hand while the fingers stay passive, which compresses the back of the wrist joint. Spreading the fingers and pressing firmly through the knuckles, especially at the base of the index finger, redistributes the load.

How do you modify yoga for bad wrists?

Make fists and work on the knuckles, come onto the forearms, use a wedge or a folded mat edge under the heel of the hand to reduce the angle, or take weight-bearing out entirely and practise standing and seated postures instead.

Can yoga cause carpal tunnel syndrome?

Yoga does not typically cause it, and gentle practice is often recommended alongside treatment. But if you have numbness, tingling or pins and needles in the fingers, see a doctor rather than adjusting your downward dog.

How do you strengthen wrists for yoga?

Gradually and often, rather than heavily. Short holds in tabletop, moving weight forward and back over the hands, finger and knuckle presses, and simply spending more time on the hands in small doses will build tolerance over weeks.
