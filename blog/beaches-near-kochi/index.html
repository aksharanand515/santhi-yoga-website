Fort Kochi & Kerala

The best beaches near Kochi, from Fort Kochi

Fort Kochi sits on the sea, but its own beach is better for walking than for swimming. Within an hour there are long, clean beaches where you can actually get in the water.

By Master Aksharanand A S, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 25 October 2026 5 min read

In short

The best beach near Kochi for swimming is Cherai, about 25–30 km north of Fort Kochi on Vypin island, reached by ferry and bus or auto in around an hour. Kuzhupilly, on the way, is quieter. Marari, about 55 km south, is the most beautiful and the calmest. Fort Kochi Beach itself is good for sunsets and walks, not for swimming. Swim only from October to May, and only where lifeguards are on duty.

On our 28-day courses the free day each week almost always ends at the sea, so these are beaches we know well. Before anything else, read our page on Fort Kochi Beach, because it explains why the town’s own shoreline is not the place to swim.

Cherai Beach

Cherai is the one most people mean when they ask about a beach near Kochi. It runs along the northern end of Vypin island with the Arabian Sea on one side and the backwaters only a few hundred metres behind it. The sand is fine, the slope is gentle, and in the dry season the sea is often calm enough to swim.

Lifeguards are posted at the main access point, which is where you should swim. Weekdays are quiet; weekend afternoons bring families from Ernakulam. There are simple seafood places along the road and a few cafés for a vegetarian lunch.

Getting there: take the ferry from Fort Kochi to Vypin, a few minutes across the harbour mouth. From the Vypin side, buses run north along the island to Cherai through the day, or you can take an auto-rickshaw. Allow about an hour door to door. A taxi from Fort Kochi takes roughly the same time by road.

Kuzhupilly Beach

On the same road a little before Cherai, Kuzhupilly is smaller and almost entirely local. There is less to buy and fewer people, which is exactly the appeal. Swim only on very calm days and stay close to shore.

Puthuvype and the lighthouse

Just across the ferry from Fort Kochi, Puthuvype has a long beach and one of the tallest lighthouses in Kerala. It is a pleasant late-afternoon trip for the view, though the currents near the harbour mouth make it a poor place to swim.

Kannamaly, Chellanam and Andhakaranazhi

South of Fort Kochi the coast is lined with fishing villages protected by stone seawalls. Kannamaly and Chellanam are for watching boats come in rather than for lying on the sand.

Further south, Andhakaranazhi is where a backwater channel opens into the sea. It is striking at sunset and quiet on weekdays, but the current at the estuary is strong, so admire the water rather than entering it.

Marari Beach

Marari, near Mararikulam on the way to Alappuzha, is the most beautiful beach within reach of Kochi: wide, clean, lined with coconut palms and a scattering of quiet resorts, and still a working fishing beach. It is far enough that most people make a day of it or stay a night. It combines well with the backwaters; see our guide to the Alleppey backwaters.

Getting there: about 1.5 hours by taxi, or a train or bus to Mararikulam or Alappuzha and a short auto ride.

When it is safe to swim

The Arabian Sea off Kerala is not a lagoon. From June to September the monsoon brings big swells and strong rip currents, and swimming at any of these beaches is dangerous. Every year there are drownings, usually of visitors.

  • Swim from October to May, and then only on calm days.
  • Swim where lifeguards are on duty, and obey their flags and whistles.
  • Never swim at estuaries or harbour mouths, where currents are strongest.
  • Do not swim after a heavy meal or in the hottest part of the day.

What to wear, and beach etiquette

Kerala’s beaches are family places. Swimwear is fine in the water at Cherai and Marari, but cover up with a sarong or shorts and a T-shirt on the sand and on the way to lunch. Topless sunbathing is not acceptable anywhere in India. Our page on travelling Fort Kochi as a solo woman has more practical advice.

A beach day around your practice

A good rhythm, and the one many of our students follow: the 7:30 am Hatha class, a quick breakfast, the ferry to Vypin by 10:30, a swim and a long lunch at Cherai, and back in Fort Kochi for the sunset. If you are here for longer, our daily yoga classes in Fort Kochi run every morning and afternoon, so a beach day costs you only one of them.

Common questions

Which is the best beach near Kochi?

For swimming, Cherai, about 25 to 30 km north of Fort Kochi. For beauty and calm, Marari, about 55 km south. Fort Kochi Beach is best for a sunset walk.

How far is Cherai Beach from Fort Kochi?

About 25 to 30 km. With the Vypin ferry and a bus or auto-rickshaw it takes about an hour.

Can you swim at Fort Kochi Beach?

It is not recommended. The water is shallow and murky, there are currents near the harbour mouth, and fishing nets line the shore. Go to Cherai or Marari to swim.

Is Cherai Beach safe for swimming?

Usually, from October to May on calm days, at the lifeguard station. Do not swim during the June to September monsoon.

How do I get from Fort Kochi to Cherai by public transport?

Take the ferry from Fort Kochi to Vypin, then one of the frequent buses north along Vypin island to Cherai, or an auto-rickshaw from the jetty.
