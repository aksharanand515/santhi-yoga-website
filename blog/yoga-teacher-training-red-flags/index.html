Teacher training

Red flags: how to spot a poor yoga teacher training

Most trainings are run honestly. The ones that are not tend to give themselves away early, in the wording of a brochure and the shape of an answer.

By Master Aksharanand A S, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 25 September 2026 7 min read

In short

The clearest warning signs are a lead teacher who is never named, group sizes that are not disclosed, a syllabus the school will not send, prices with vague extras, pressure to pay a large deposit immediately, guaranteed certification for everyone, reviews that all appear in the same week, and no way to speak to a past graduate. A good school answers all of these in one email, with numbers.

A 200-hour training costs a month of your life and a meaningful amount of money. The difference between a good one and a bad one is rarely visible on a website, which is designed to look the same either way. It becomes visible the moment you ask specific questions.

Here are the signals that repeatedly turn out to matter, and what an honest school says instead. The 12-point checklist is the positive version of this list, written as an email you can send.

The teacher is never named

If a site talks about "our experienced team of certified instructors" without naming the person who will lead your month, ask who it is. Then ask whether they teach the whole course, or drop in for a session and leave.

What good looks like: a named lead teacher, their lineage and training, how long they have taught, and a clear answer about how many hours a day they are actually in the room.

Group size is a mystery

Group size decides how much of the month is about you. In a group of forty, a teacher cannot watch your alignment, remember your knee, or hear your teaching practice. Schools running big batches rarely publish the number.

What good looks like: "Twelve to sixteen students, one lead teacher, one assistant." Ours runs small on purpose; there is more about why in why small groups matter.

No syllabus, or one with no hours

A real syllabus shows hours against the four subjects: practice, methodology, anatomy, philosophy. A list of inspirational words is not a syllabus. Be wary too of a course that hits 200 hours only by counting meals, free time and travel days.

What good looks like: a document, sent the same day, with hours you can add up.

Money is vague or urgent

  • "All-inclusive" without a list. Inclusive of what? Food, all meals or two? Airport pickup? Excursions? Manuals? Certificate?
  • Extras that appear later: a certificate fee, a manual fee, a "registration" fee on arrival.
  • Pressure. "Only two places left, pay today." A school that manufactures urgency will manufacture other things.
  • Large non-refundable deposits with no written cancellation policy.
  • Cash only, or a request to pay an individual rather than the school.

What good looks like: one price, a written list of what it includes and excludes, a stated deposit and a cancellation policy you can read before paying. Ours is €899 all-inclusive for 28 days, and the page for our yoga teacher training in Kerala lists what that covers. The cost breakdown article explains the extras to watch for across the market.

Promises that cannot be kept

  • "Guaranteed certification." If nobody can fail, the certificate is a receipt.
  • "Become a yoga teacher in 28 days" with no practice prerequisite at all. A month makes a beginner teacher, and only if you arrive with a practice.
  • "Job placement guaranteed" or promises of studio work abroad.
  • Health claims: curing conditions, fixing depression, healing injuries.
  • Vague accreditation language such as "internationally certified", which means nothing by itself. If a school is registered somewhere, it can name the registry; if it is not, it should say so, as we do about Yoga Alliance.

Reviews that do not add up

Look at dates and detail. Twenty five-star reviews posted in one week, all short and all similar, are worth less than five detailed ones spread across a year that mention the teacher by name and describe the room. Check more than one site, and see whether the school replies.

What good looks like: reviews across years, on Google and elsewhere, with specifics — and a school happy to put you in touch with two recent graduates. If you are travelling alone, our guide for solo women adds the questions about rooms, touch and boundaries.

Photographs of somewhere else

Stock photographs of a beach in Bali on the page for a course in Kerala tell you the school is selling a feeling rather than a place. Reverse-image-search one or two. Ask for a short video of the actual practice hall and rooms, filmed today. A real school sends it.

What a good answer looks like

Send one email with five questions: who teaches, how many students, what the syllabus hours are, what the price includes and excludes, and may I speak to a recent graduate. Then judge the reply.

A good school answers all five, in numbers, within a day or two, and offers a call. A poor one answers the easy ones, repeats the brochure, and pushes you towards a deposit. You will know within one exchange, and it costs you nothing but the email.

If you are comparing places as well as schools, Kerala or Rishikesh covers the differences honestly, and what actually happens in a 28-day course describes the month itself.

Common questions

What are the warning signs of a bad yoga teacher training?

No named lead teacher, undisclosed group size, no syllabus with hours, vague pricing or hidden extras, pressure to pay a deposit immediately, guaranteed certification, clustered reviews and no contact with past graduates.

How many students should a 200-hour yoga teacher training have?

Small groups, roughly twelve to twenty with one lead teacher, allow real attention and teaching practice. Above about thirty, individual correction and feedback on your teaching become very limited.

Is a guaranteed certificate a bad sign?

Yes. If every student passes regardless of attendance, safety and teaching ability, the certificate records that you attended and paid, not that you are ready to teach.

What should a yoga teacher training price include?

Ask for it in writing. A clear all-inclusive price usually covers tuition, accommodation, all meals, manuals and the certificate, and states what is excluded, such as flights, visa and personal excursions.
