The meaning of the word, its three foundational sources, the question at its heart, its main schools, its roots just outside Kochi, and why it sits in the name of our school.

In short

**Vedanta is one of India’s classical schools of philosophy, based on the Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita and the Brahma Sutras.** Its central question is the relationship between the individual self, *atman*, and ultimate reality, *Brahman*. Its best-known school, Advaita, was taught by Adi Shankara, who was born at Kaladi in Kerala, about 37 km from Kochi.

Our school is called Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies, and people often ask what the second half means. Yoga is familiar; Vedanta is not. Yet for many traditional teachers, including the Sivananda lineage I trained in, the two belong together: yoga as the practice, Vedanta as the understanding behind it.

This is a plain-English introduction, not a scholarly one. If you are interested in how Vedanta shapes the teaching of yoga, see also [Sivananda yoga in Kochi](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/sivananda-yoga-kochi/), where Vedanta appears in the fifth of the five points of yoga.

## What the word means

Vedanta joins Veda, the ancient scriptures, and anta, end. It means both the final part of the Vedas — the Upanishads — and their culmination: the philosophical insight they point towards. It is one of the six classical schools of Indian philosophy, alongside Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga and Mimamsa.

## The three foundational sources

| Source | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **The Upanishads** | Philosophical dialogues at the end of the Vedas, exploring the nature of the self and reality |
| **The Bhagavad Gita** | The dialogue between Krishna and Arjuna in the Mahabharata, on duty, action, devotion and knowledge |
| **The Brahma Sutras** | Concise aphorisms that systematise the teaching of the Upanishads |

## The question at its heart

Vedanta asks what we really are. Its answer turns on two terms: atman, the innermost self, and Brahman, the ultimate reality underlying everything. Much of our suffering, it says, comes from mistaking ourselves for what changes — the body, the thoughts, the roles we play — rather than recognising the awareness in which all of these appear. Seeing this clearly is moksha, liberation.

## The main schools

| School | Key teacher | Core view |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Advaita** — non-dualism | Adi Shankara | The self and ultimate reality are not two |
| **Vishishtadvaita** — qualified non-dualism | Ramanuja | The self is real and distinct, yet inseparable from the divine |
| **Dvaita** — dualism | Madhva | The self and the divine are eternally distinct |

## Adi Shankara and Kerala

Advaita Vedanta’s most famous teacher, **Adi Shankara**, was born at **Kaladi** in Ernakulam district, on the banks of the Periyar river, traditionally in the eighth century. Kaladi is about 37 km from Kochi and close to Cochin International Airport. From there, Shankara is said to have travelled the length of India, writing commentaries on the Upanishads, the Gita and the Brahma Sutras and founding monasteries that still exist. His birthplace can be visited today — an easy half-day trip for anyone practising in Fort Kochi.

## Vedanta and yoga

Yoga and Vedanta are distinct schools, but they have long been practised together. Put simply, yoga steadies the body, breath and mind; Vedanta explains what is found when the mind is steady.

- The **Bhagavad Gita** describes paths of action (karma yoga), devotion (bhakti yoga) and knowledge (jnana yoga).
- In the **Sivananda tradition**, the fifth point of yoga — positive thinking and meditation — draws directly on Vedanta.
- **Meditation**, the goal of [the eight limbs of yoga](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/eight-limbs-of-yoga/), is where the two meet.

## Vedanta in everyday life

- **Witnessing.** Noticing thoughts and moods as things that come and go, rather than as who you are.
- **Discrimination**, viveka: telling the lasting from the passing.
- **Non-attachment**, vairagya: acting fully without clinging to outcomes.
- **Self-inquiry.** Asking, patiently, “Who am I?”

## Common questions

### What does Vedanta mean?

Vedanta combines Veda, the ancient scriptures, and anta, end. It refers to the Upanishads at the end of the Vedas and to the philosophy that culminates from them.

### What is Advaita Vedanta?

Advaita, or non-dualism, is the school of Vedanta which teaches that the individual self (atman) and ultimate reality (Brahman) are not two. Its best-known teacher is Adi Shankara.

### Where was Adi Shankara born?

At Kaladi in Ernakulam district, Kerala, traditionally in the eighth century. Kaladi is about 37 km from Kochi and near Cochin International Airport.

### Is Vedanta a religion?

Vedanta is a philosophical school within the Hindu tradition. Its questions about the nature of the self and reality are studied by people of many backgrounds, and it can be approached as philosophy.

### What is the difference between yoga and Vedanta?

Both are among the six classical schools of Indian philosophy. Yoga emphasises practice and discipline to steady the mind; Vedanta emphasises understanding the nature of the self. Many traditions teach them together.
