# *Brahma muhurta*: the yogic hour before sunrise

Yoga and Ayurveda both recommend waking before dawn. The tradition is very old, the reasons are practical as well as spiritual, and there is a right and a wrong way to do it.

By **Master Aksharanand A S**, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 7 October 2026 5 min read

In short

Brahma muhurta is a traditional 48-minute period that begins about 96 minutes before sunrise and ends 48 minutes before it. Yoga and Ayurveda treat it as the best time for meditation, study and practice, because the mind is clear and the world is quiet. In Kochi it starts between about 4:30 and 5:10 am depending on the season. Waking early only helps if you also sleep early enough to get seven or more hours, so shift your bedtime first.

Waking before sunrise is the first instruction of [dinacharya, the Ayurvedic daily routine](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/dinacharya-ayurvedic-daily-routine/), and the start of the day in many ashrams in India. This article explains the idea, the timing and the honest science behind it.

## What is brahma muhurta?

A *muhurta* is a traditional Indian unit of time: one-thirtieth of a day and night, or 48 minutes. *Brahma* here refers to the creator, or to Brahman, the absolute. Brahma muhurta is usually translated as “the creator’s hour” or “the time of Brahman”.

It is the second-to-last muhurta of the night: the 48 minutes that begin roughly an hour and a half before sunrise. Classical Ayurveda opens its daily routine here. The *Ashtanga Hridaya*, one of the main Ayurvedic texts, begins its chapter on the daily regimen with the instruction *brahme muhurte uttishthet*: rise in brahma muhurta.

## How do you work out the time of brahma muhurta?

It depends on sunrise where you are, so it shifts through the year.

1. Find today’s sunrise time for your town.
2. Subtract 96 minutes. That is when brahma muhurta begins.
3. It ends 48 minutes later, which is 48 minutes before sunrise.

| Season | Sunrise | Brahma muhurta |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **May–June** | About 6:05 am | About 4:30–5:15 am |
| **March and September** | About 6:20 am | About 4:45–5:30 am |
| **December–January** | About 6:45 am | About 5:10–5:55 am |

Near the equator, as in Kerala, the time barely moves. Far north or south, it can swing by hours, which is one reason many teachers simply say “wake before dawn” and leave the arithmetic aside.

## Why do yogis practise before dawn?

The traditional reasons are a mix of the spiritual and the practical.

- **The mind is clearer.** After sleep, before the day’s messages and decisions, thinking is quieter. The texts call this a *sattvic* time, dominated by clarity. Our guide to [the three gunas](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/gunas-explained/) explains the term.
- **The world is quiet.** Few people are awake, there is little noise, and nobody needs you yet. In a Kerala town, it is the one hour before the traffic, the temple loudspeakers and the heat.
- **The body is fresh and the stomach empty,** which is how pranayama and meditation are traditionally practised.
- **It protects the practice.** A practice done first thing happens. A practice saved for later is the first thing a busy day removes.
- **In Ayurveda,** the hours before dawn are a *vata* time, associated with lightness and movement, considered good for the subtle work of meditation. See [the three doshas](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/doshas-explained/).

## What does sleep science say about waking early?

Here the evidence supports part of the tradition and warns against a careless version of it.

- **Earlier sleep timing may protect mood.** A 2021 genetic study in *JAMA Psychiatry*, using data from about 840,000 people, found that a genetic tendency to sleep one hour earlier was associated with a 23% lower risk of major depression. It suggests timing matters, not only duration.
- **Morning light helps set the body clock.** Getting outside in daylight early in the day helps anchor the circadian rhythm, which tends to make it easier to fall asleep at night.
- **Sleep length still matters most.** Sleep organisations recommend at least seven hours a night for adults. Waking at 4:30 am after going to bed at midnight is not brahma muhurta. It is sleep deprivation.
- **Chronotype is real.** People are genuinely different. Some are natural early risers; some are not, and can shift only so far. Forcing a strong night owl to wake at 4:30 am may do more harm than good.

So the honest summary: an early, consistent schedule with enough sleep is good for most people. The early alarm is only half of it. The early bedtime is the other half.

## What should you do during brahma muhurta?

Traditionally, the quiet, inward practices. A simple forty-minute sequence:

| Minutes | Practice |
| --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Wake, wash the face, drink a glass of water, use the toilet |
| 5–10 | A few gentle stretches, or two slow rounds of [sun salutations](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/surya-namaskar-steps/) |
| 10–20 | Pranayama: slow breathing, then [alternate nostril breathing](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/nadi-shodhana-alternate-nostril-breathing/) |
| 20–40 | Meditation or mantra repetition. See [how to meditate every day](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/how-to-meditate-daily/) and [japa with a mala](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/japa-mala-108/) |
| 40+ | A few minutes of reading or reflection, before the phone |

Leave the phone in another room until you have finished. That one change does more for a morning practice than any technique. Our guide on [how to sit for meditation](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/how-to-sit-for-meditation/) helps if sitting is uncomfortable at that hour.

## How do you start waking up earlier?

1. **Move bedtime first.** Go to bed fifteen minutes earlier each week, and wake fifteen minutes earlier. Jumping two hours at once rarely lasts.
2. **Keep the same time every day,** weekends included. Consistency matters more than the exact hour.
3. **Eat dinner early and light,** at least two to three hours before bed. Our [evening practice for sleep](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/yoga-for-better-sleep/) helps you wind down.
4. **Dim screens and lights** in the last hour before bed.
5. **Get daylight soon after waking,** or as soon as the sun is up.
6. **Have one reason to get up:** a practice you look forward to, not just an alarm.

Expect three or four weeks before it feels natural. If you are staying in Fort Kochi, the 7:30 am class gives your early morning a destination, with time before it for your own quiet practice.

## When is early rising not right for you?

- **Shift workers** and anyone with an irregular schedule. Consistency and enough sleep matter more than the clock.
- **New parents,** whose sleep is already broken.
- **People with insomnia, depression or other health conditions,** who should change their sleep schedule only with medical advice.
- **Strong night owls,** who may do better with a consistent, slightly earlier schedule than with a 4:30 am alarm.

The spirit of brahma muhurta is a quiet, unhurried start to the day, given to practice. If that happens at 6:30 for you, you have kept the spirit.

## Common questions

**What time is brahma muhurta?**

It begins about 96 minutes before sunrise and lasts 48 minutes, ending 48 minutes before sunrise. In Kochi, Kerala, it starts between about 4:30 and 5:10 am depending on the season. Work it out from your own local sunrise time.

**What should you do in brahma muhurta?**

Traditionally, quiet inward practices: pranayama, meditation, mantra repetition, prayer or study. Gentle stretching first is fine. Keep the phone away until you have finished.

**Is waking up at brahma muhurta healthy?**

For most people an early, consistent schedule is healthy, and a 2021 genetic study linked earlier sleep timing with a lower risk of depression. But it only helps if you also go to bed early enough to sleep at least seven hours.

**Why is brahma muhurta considered special?**

Yoga and Ayurveda describe it as a sattvic time, when the mind is clear and calm and the world is quiet, making it ideal for meditation and study. Ayurveda’s Ashtanga Hridaya opens its daily routine with the instruction to rise at this time.

**How do I start waking up at 4:30 am?**

Move your bedtime and wake time fifteen minutes earlier each week, keep the same times every day, eat dinner early and light, and dim screens before bed. Expect three to four weeks before it feels natural.
