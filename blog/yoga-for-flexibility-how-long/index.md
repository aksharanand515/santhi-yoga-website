# Yoga for flexibility, and *how long it really takes*

Almost everyone who walks into a first class says the same sentence: I am not flexible. Here is what actually changes, how quickly, and what makes it take longer than it needs to.

By **Master Aksharanand A S**, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 23 September 2026 5 min read

![A teacher guiding a student into a deep backbend in the garden pavilion](https://santhiyogaindia.com/assets/img/hands-on-backbend-adjustment-garden-pavilion-800.webp)

Range comes from repetition and patience, not from being pushed.

In short

**Most people notice a difference within two to three weeks of practising three times a week, see a clear change in two to three months, and keep improving for years.** The early gains come mostly from your nervous system allowing more range, not from tissue lengthening — which is why a week off costs less than people fear.

Flexibility is the reason most beginners give for not starting, which is a little like refusing to learn to read until you can read. It is the outcome, not the entry requirement.

## How long, realistically

- **First two or three weeks:** movements feel easier, and the first few minutes of practice stop feeling like a negotiation.
- **Two to three months:** visible change — hands further down the legs, a straighter seated position, easier breathing in postures.
- **Six months to a year:** range you keep without thinking about, and postures that were impossible become ordinary.
- **Beyond that:** slower, deeper change, and a great deal less interest in whether your hands touch the floor.

Age matters less than consistency. Some of the stiffest people who have walked into our pavilion have also been the most changed by a year of practice, because they showed up.

## What is actually changing

Early on, very little in the muscle itself. What changes first is tolerance: your nervous system learns that a given range is safe and stops guarding against it. That is why you can be looser at the end of a class than the start, and why warm rooms feel easier.

With months of regular practice, longer-term adaptations follow in muscle and connective tissue. This is slow work. It is also why holding a posture for several breaths, as traditional Hatha practice does, works better than passing through it once.

## How often to practise

Three times a week changes things. Twice a week maintains. Once a week is a pleasant habit that will not move you much. Daily is wonderful if your life allows it, provided some of those days are gentle.

Short and frequent beats long and rare. Twenty minutes four times a week does more than two hours every other Sunday.

## What slows people down

- **Forcing.** Pushing into pain makes the nervous system guard harder, so you get less range, not more.
- **Bouncing.** Ballistic stretching has its place in sport; it is not how this practice works.
- **Holding the breath.** If you cannot breathe evenly in a posture, you are too deep in it.
- **Comparison.** Hip sockets differ from person to person. Some bodies will never reach some shapes, and that is anatomy rather than failure.
- **Stopping and restarting.** Six weeks on, six weeks off, repeatedly, keeps you at the beginning.

## A simple week

1. Two longer practices — a full class, or an hour at home.
2. Two short ones — twenty minutes, hips and hamstrings, or shoulders if you sit at a desk.
3. One day of nothing but walking.
4. Every session: breathe slowly, hold positions for five to eight breaths, and stop while it still feels good.

## Sensation and pain

Stretch should feel like a broad, dull pull in the belly of a muscle that eases as you breathe. Sharp, pinching, burning, electrical or joint-line pain is a stop signal, not a challenge. If something hurts after practice for more than a day, or you have an existing injury, see a physiotherapist or doctor — and tell your teacher before class so the practice can be adapted around you.

## Common questions

**How long does it take to become flexible with yoga?**

Most people notice a difference within two to three weeks of practising three times a week, and see clear change in two to three months. Improvement continues for years, but the early progress is the most obvious.

**How often should I do yoga to get more flexible?**

Three sessions a week is the point at which most people see steady change. Two maintains what you have. Short, frequent practice works better than one long session a week.

**Am I too stiff or too old to start yoga?**

No. Flexibility is the result of practice, not a requirement for it. Beginners, stiff bodies, older bodies and people returning after injury are welcome in every class we teach — tell the teacher first and the practice adapts.

**Should stretching hurt?**

No. A good stretch is a broad, dull sensation in the muscle that eases as you breathe. Sharp, pinching or joint pain means stop. Pain that lasts more than a day after practice is worth showing to a physiotherapist or doctor.
