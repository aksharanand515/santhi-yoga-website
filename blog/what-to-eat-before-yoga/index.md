# What to eat before yoga, and *how long to wait*

The traditional instruction is to practise on an empty stomach, which is easy advice to give and harder to follow if your class is at six in the evening.

By **Master Aksharanand A S**, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 27 September 2026 5 min read

![A shared vegetarian meal laid out for yoga students](https://santhiyogaindia.com/assets/img/shared-vegetarian-meal-yoga-students-800.webp)

What we serve students, and when, is part of the teaching.

In short

**Leave three to four hours after a full meal, one to two after something light, and twenty to thirty minutes after a piece of fruit or a glass of something warm.** Breathing practices, twists and inversions in particular need an empty stomach. If you cannot manage that, eat less rather than eating earlier-but-the-same.

Students ask this more than almost anything else, and usually apologetically, as though it were a small question. It is not. Getting it wrong is the most common reason a class feels worse than it should.

## Why it matters

Digestion needs blood and space. Practice asks for both.

A full stomach makes forward folds uncomfortable, twists unpleasant and inversions genuinely unwise. It also makes [pranayama](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/pranayama-for-beginners/) difficult in a specific way: the diaphragm cannot move down freely when there is a full stomach beneath it, so the breath stays shallow no matter how carefully you work at it.

Practices involving the abdomen are the strictest of all. [Kapalabhati](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/kapalabhati-breathing/), uddiyana bandha and anything with breath retention are traditionally done on an empty stomach, and that is not a matter of etiquette.

## How long to wait

- **A full meal** — rice, curry, bread, anything substantial: three to four hours.
- **Something light** — a small snack, yoghurt, a handful of nuts: one to two hours.
- **Fruit, or a warm drink**: twenty to thirty minutes.

These are not precise numbers and they vary with the person. The useful test is whether you can take a slow, full breath into the belly without resistance. If you can, you are ready.

## What to eat if you must

Some people genuinely cannot practise hungry — low blood sugar is not a discipline problem. The answer is to eat small and simple rather than to skip.

Two or three dates. Half a banana. A few soaked almonds. A warm drink, unsweetened or lightly sweetened. All of these settle quickly and none of them sit heavily.

What to avoid beforehand: anything fried, anything heavy with dairy, large quantities of raw salad, and — though it is a shame in Kerala — a full breakfast of appam and stew. Save that for after.

## Early morning classes

Morning is the easiest time to get this right, because you have already fasted overnight. Our 7.30 class is almost always practised empty, and students who arrive worried about it usually find by the second day that they prefer it.

If you wake genuinely hollow, have something small on rising and let the thirty minutes pass while you wash and walk to the shala. That is enough.

## Water, coffee and tea

Sip water rather than drinking a glass. A stomach full of water behaves much like a stomach full of food in a twist.

Coffee is a personal matter. It does not break the empty-stomach principle in any meaningful way, but on an empty stomach it makes some people jittery, and a jittery nervous system is poor ground for breathing practice. If you drink it, drink it early.

Warm water with a little lemon, or a light herbal tea, is the traditional preference and is genuinely pleasant before a morning practice.

## Eating afterwards

Wait twenty to thirty minutes after practice before a proper meal, and take the opportunity — the appetite after a good class is one of the reliable pleasures of a regular practice.

What to eat then is a longer subject, and [sattvic food](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/sattvic-food-explained/) covers the thinking behind what yoga schools serve and why.

## Common questions

**How long before yoga should you eat?**

Three to four hours after a full meal, one to two hours after a light snack, and twenty to thirty minutes after fruit or a warm drink. Pranayama, twists and inversions are the practices most affected by a full stomach.

**Can you do yoga on an empty stomach?**

Yes, and for most practices it is preferable. If you feel genuinely faint rather than simply hungry, a few dates, a banana or a warm drink half an hour beforehand is enough to steady you without filling the stomach.

**What should you eat before a morning yoga class?**

Ideally nothing. If you need something, keep it to fruit or a warm drink twenty to thirty minutes before. Morning is the easiest time to practise empty because the overnight fast has already done the work.

**Can you drink water before yoga?**

Small amounts, yes. A large volume of water sits in the stomach much like food does and makes twists and inversions uncomfortable. Sip rather than drink, and save the larger glass for afterwards.
