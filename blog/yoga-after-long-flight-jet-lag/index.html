Yoga after a long flight: easing jet lag and stiffness

What a long journey does to the body, simple things to do on the plane, how to spend your first 24 hours in Kochi, a ten-minute routine for arrival day, and when to join your first class.

By Master Aksharanand A S, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 17 September 2026 4 min read

In short

After a long flight, gentle movement, daylight and water help more than a hard workout. Walk, do slow stretches for the hips, back and neck, practise calm breathing, and get outside in the morning light to help your body clock adjust. In Kochi, a slow traditional class on your first or second morning is a good way to reset.

Plenty of people looking for a yoga class in Fort Kochi have only just arrived, often after a long journey with at least one connection. They are stiff, a little dazed, and unsure whether yoga is a good idea when they are this tired. Done gently, it usually is.

This guide covers the journey itself and the first days after it. For our class times and what a class involves, see the guide to yoga in Fort Kochi.

What a long flight does to the body

  • Hours of sitting shorten the hip flexors and hamstrings and load the lower back.
  • Dry cabin air leaves you more dehydrated than you feel.
  • Broken sleep leaves the nervous system on edge.
  • A shifted body clock — jet lag — makes you sleepy and alert at the wrong times, often for several days.

Yoga cannot remove jet lag, but it can ease the stiffness, calm the nervous system and give each day a regular anchor, which helps.

On the plane

  • Stand and walk in the aisle every hour or two when it is safe to do so.
  • In your seat: ankle circles, pointing and flexing the feet, and lifting the heels, a few minutes at a time.
  • Gentle seated twist and slow shoulder rolls to ease the back and neck.
  • Drink water regularly, and go easy on alcohol.
  • Slow breathing — a longer out-breath than in-breath — helps you rest.

If you have a history of blood clots or other risk factors, ask your doctor about precautions for long flights before you travel.

Your first 24 hours in Kochi

  1. Get daylight, especially in the morning. Light is the strongest signal for resetting your body clock. A slow walk along the Fort Kochi waterfront is ideal.
  2. Eat lightly at local mealtimes, and drink plenty of water; Kerala’s heat adds to the dehydration of the flight.
  3. Keep naps short, if you need one at all, and avoid sleeping through the afternoon.
  4. Do ten minutes of gentle yoga before bed — see below.
  5. Go to bed at a local, reasonable hour.

Keep everything slow and easy. If you have high blood pressure, glaucoma or are pregnant, skip legs up the wall and ask your doctor what suits you. More breathing practices are in pranayama for beginners.

Common questions

Does yoga help with jet lag?

Yoga cannot remove jet lag, but gentle practice eases stiffness from sitting, calms the nervous system and gives the day a regular anchor. Daylight, water and sleeping at local times matter most.

What yoga poses help after a long flight?

Gentle, restful ones: legs up the wall, knees to chest, cat–cow, child’s pose and a gentle lying twist, followed by slow breathing such as humming bee breath.

Should I exercise hard after a long flight?

Usually not on the first day. A walk in daylight and gentle stretching are more helpful than a hard workout while you are dehydrated and short of sleep.

What is the time difference between India and the UK?

India is 4 hours 30 minutes ahead of the UK during British summer time and 5 hours 30 minutes ahead in winter. India does not change its clocks.

Can I join a yoga class the day I arrive in Kochi?

Yes, if you feel up to it. At Santhi Yoga, tell the teacher you have just flown in, rest whenever you need to, and message us the evening before if you plan to come to the 7:30 am class.

What exercises can I do on a plane?

Walk the aisle every hour or two when safe, and in your seat do ankle circles, heel lifts, gentle twists and shoulder rolls. Drink water regularly.
