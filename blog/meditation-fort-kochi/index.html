Meditation in Fort Kochi and Kochi: how to start, and where to learn

The kinds of meditation you will come across, a ten-minute practice you can begin today, the obstacles everyone meets, and the honest range of places to learn in Kochi and Kerala.

By Master Aksharanand A S, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 17 September 2026 5 min read

In short

To start meditating, sit comfortably for ten minutes a day, at the same time, and rest your attention on the breath, returning gently each time the mind wanders. To learn with guidance in Kochi, join a yoga class that includes breathing and stillness, take a workshop or structured course, or, for real depth, a residential Vipassana course in Kerala.

Many people come to meditation sideways. They join a yoga class for their back or their flexibility, and discover that the part they remember afterwards is the stillness at the end. Then they search "meditation Fort Kochi" and find surprisingly little.

Meditation is simpler to begin than most people think, and harder to keep up than they expect. This guide gives you a practice to start today and an honest map of where to learn more. If you want to try it inside a yoga class first, our guide to yoga in Fort Kochi has class times.

A ten-minute practice to start today

  1. Choose a time you can keep every day. Early morning, before the phone, is easiest for most people.
  2. Sit on something stable: a cushion on the floor, or a chair with your feet flat. The back upright but not stiff.
  3. Set a timer for ten minutes, so you are not checking the clock.
  4. Close your eyes and notice the breath where you feel it most clearly — at the nostrils or the belly.
  5. Count breaths from one to ten, then start again at one.
  6. When you lose count — and you will, constantly — notice that the mind wandered, and begin again at one. That return is the practice.
  7. Before opening your eyes, sit for a few breaths without counting.

Do this daily for two weeks before judging it. Ten minutes every day does more than an hour once a week.

Why yoga traditionally comes before meditation

In the yoga tradition, postures and breathing are not separate from meditation; they are preparation for it. Asana makes the body comfortable enough to sit still. Pranayama, breath control, steadies the nervous system — our beginner’s guide to pranayama teaches six practices. Only then does concentration — dharana — deepen into meditation, dhyana.

That is why many people find it easier to meditate at the end of a yoga class than on a cushion at home: the class has already done half the work. The Sun Salutation, practised slowly with the breath, is itself a moving meditation.

Common obstacles, and what helps

The obstacles almost everyone meets
ObstacleWhat helps
“My mind won’t stop.”It is not supposed to stop. Noticing and returning is the whole practice.
SleepinessSit more upright, open the eyes slightly, meditate earlier in the day.
Back or knee pain when sittingSit higher, lean against a wall, or use a chair. Cross-legged is not required.
No timeFive minutes daily beats thirty minutes occasionally.
“Am I doing it right?”If you sat and kept coming back, you did it right.

Meditation helps many people feel calmer and more focused. It is not a substitute for medical care: if you are living with anxiety, depression or trauma, talk to a doctor or therapist, and let a meditation teacher know.

Common questions

Are there meditation classes in Fort Kochi?

Yes. We run a separate meditation class in Fort Kochi; its timing depends on the season and availability, so message us to ask when the next one is. Our daily yoga classes also include breathing practice and a deep final relaxation.

How long should a beginner meditate?

Start with ten minutes a day, at the same time each day. Regularity matters more than length. Increase gradually once ten minutes feels settled.

Is Vipassana free in Kerala?

Yes. Dhamma Ketana, the main Vipassana centre in Kerala, in Alappuzha district, charges nothing for its 10-day courses, including food and accommodation. They are run entirely on donations from past students.

Do I need to sit cross-legged to meditate?

No. Any stable, upright seat works, including a chair with your feet flat on the floor. Comfort matters more than the shape of the legs.

What is the difference between meditation and pranayama?

Pranayama is the active regulation of the breath through specific techniques. Meditation is sustained, quiet attention. In the yoga tradition, pranayama is practised first because it steadies the mind for meditation.

Can meditation help with anxiety?

Many people find regular meditation helps them feel calmer. It is not a replacement for medical or psychological care, so anyone with an anxiety disorder should speak to a doctor or therapist, and tell their meditation teacher.
