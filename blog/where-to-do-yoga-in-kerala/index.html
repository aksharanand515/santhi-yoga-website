Fort Kochi & Kerala

Where to do yoga in Kerala: Fort Kochi, Varkala, Kovalam, Munnar and beyond

The main places to practise in Kerala compared honestly by setting, season and style — so you can match the place to the kind of yoga holiday you actually want.

By Master Aksharanand A S, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 17 September 2026 4 min read

In short

Choose Varkala for clifftop and beach yoga in the dry season, Kovalam and the backwaters for Ayurveda-led retreats, Munnar for cool hills, Neyyar Dam for the official Sivananda ashram, and Fort Kochi for daily walk-in classes in a historic town close to the airport. The right place depends on the season, the style of practice and how you want to spend time off the mat.

“Where should I do yoga in Kerala?” is one of the questions we are asked most, usually by someone who has already found five retreats in five different towns. The honest answer is that Kerala’s yoga places are genuinely different from each other, and the season you travel in matters as much as the place.

We teach in Fort Kochi, so read everything here knowing that; we have tried to be fair about where other places do better. For practising with us, see our guide to yoga in Fort Kochi.

Fort Kochi

Fort Kochi is not a yoga resort; it is a real town with a long history, where yoga is taught by a small number of teachers rather than a strip of retreats. That is its strength. You can join a traditional class in the morning without committing to a package, spend the day among Portuguese churches, Jewish Mattancherry and Kathakali, and practise again the next day. It is close to Kerala’s main international airport, and because it is a working town rather than a seasonal beach resort, it does not shut down in the monsoon.

Our classes run every day at 7:30 am and 3:30 pm. Many visitors practise here at the start or end of a Kerala trip.

Varkala

Varkala is the best-known coastal yoga destination in Kerala, with cafés and retreats along a laterite cliff above the beach and plenty of schools running courses. It is lively and beautiful in the dry season, roughly November to February. In the monsoon many cliff businesses and retreats close and the sea is rough, so it is a poor choice from June to August.

Kovalam and the backwaters

Kovalam, near Trivandrum, and the backwater region around Alappuzha are the heartland of resort-based Ayurveda. Yoga here usually comes as part of a treatment package, often with a doctor’s consultation and a set diet. Choose these if Ayurvedic treatment is your main purpose and yoga is supporting it.

Neyyar Dam

The Sivananda Yoga Vedanta Dhanwantari Ashram at Neyyar Dam is the organisation’s official ashram in Kerala: residential, disciplined and structured around a full daily schedule. It suits people who want immersion in that tradition. Our guide to Sivananda yoga in Kochi explains the tradition and how to practise it closer to Kochi.

Munnar and the hills

Munnar’s tea estates and cooler climate make it a good place for quiet, nature-based retreats and a welcome escape from coastal heat. It suits walkers and people who want rest more than a busy class schedule.

Combining places on one trip

A common Kerala route runs from Kochi to Munnar, down to the backwaters, and on to Varkala or Trivandrum. Fort Kochi fits naturally at the start — to recover from the flight and learn a practice — or at the end, before flying home. A week of daily classes in Fort Kochi gives you a routine to carry through the rest of the trip. Our plan for a week of yoga in Fort Kochi shows how that week can look.

Common questions

Where is the best place to do yoga in Kerala?

It depends on the season and the experience you want. Varkala suits beach and clifftop yoga from November to February, Kovalam and the backwaters suit Ayurveda-led retreats, Munnar suits cool hill retreats, Neyyar Dam has the official Sivananda ashram, and Fort Kochi suits daily walk-in classes in a historic town.

Is Varkala or Fort Kochi better for yoga?

Varkala is better for a beach-based retreat scene in the dry season. Fort Kochi is better for traditional daily walk-in classes combined with history and culture, close to Cochin airport, and it does not close down in the monsoon.

Where is the Sivananda ashram in Kerala?

The Sivananda Yoga Vedanta Dhanwantari Ashram is at Neyyar Dam near Trivandrum, roughly 230 km south of Kochi. There is also an official Sivananda centre in Trivandrum city.

What is the best time for yoga in Kerala?

November to February is the most comfortable across Kerala. In the monsoon, from June to September, Varkala’s cliff scene largely closes, while Fort Kochi classes, ashrams and Ayurveda resorts continue.

Can I do yoga in Kerala during the monsoon?

Yes. Daily classes in Fort Kochi continue through the monsoon, as do residential ashrams and Ayurveda resorts. Beach towns such as Varkala are much quieter and many businesses close.

How far is Varkala from Kochi?

About 170 km by road, roughly three and a half hours by car.
