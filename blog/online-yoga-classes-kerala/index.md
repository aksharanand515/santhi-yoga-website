## Live, recorded, group or private

| Format | How it works | Strength | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Recorded video** | Follow along any time | Flexible, cheap or free | Nobody sees you; mistakes go uncorrected |
| **Live group class** | A scheduled class on a video call | Real teacher, routine, sense of a group | Attention shared across screens |
| **Live private session** | One-to-one on a video call | Full attention, practice built around you | Higher cost; needs a good camera set-up |

The difference that matters most is not group versus private, but **live versus recorded**. A live teacher can see that your knee is drifting inwards. A video cannot.

## Can a complete beginner start online?

Yes, with care. The risk for beginners is learning postures slightly wrong, with nobody close enough to notice, and repeating the mistake for months. Three things reduce that risk:

1. **Start with a few live private sessions**, so the teacher can see how you move before you join a group.
2. **Keep it simple.** Leave headstands and deep backbends until you have practised with a teacher in the room.
3. **Use a camera angle the teacher can actually read** — see below.

If you can get to a single in-person class before starting online, even better. The [beginner’s guide to your first class](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/first-yoga-class-beginners-guide/) covers the basics that apply either way.

## Setting up your space

| Item | Aim for |
| --- | --- |
| **Space** | The length of a mat, plus room to stretch your arms out on both sides |
| **Floor** | A non-slip mat on a hard, level floor |
| **Camera position** | Side-on, a few metres away, low down, in landscape, showing your whole body lying down and standing |
| **Light** | Facing a window or light source, not with a bright window behind you |
| **Sound** | A speaker loud enough to hear from the floor, or wireless earphones |
| **Connection** | Close to the router; other devices off the network during class |
| **Surroundings** | Furniture moved, pets and children briefed, phone notifications off |

Test the camera angle before your first class: lie down on the mat and check that your whole body is in frame. That one check solves most problems.

## Getting the most from an online class

- **Keep your camera on.** A teacher cannot correct what they cannot see.
- **Send injuries and health details beforehand** by message, so the teacher knows before class starts.
- **Log in five minutes early** to sort out sound and camera.
- **Stay for the final relaxation.** It is the part most often skipped at home, and the part you most need.
- **Practise a little between classes**, even ten minutes. That is where online learning really sticks; our [20-minute home routine](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/beginner-yoga-routine-at-home/) is a good place to start.

## Time zones

Classes from Kerala run on **Indian Standard Time, UTC+5:30**, and India does not change its clocks. As an example, an evening class at 6:30 pm in India is 2:00 pm in London and 9:00 am in New York during their summer time, and 11:00 pm in Sydney during its winter. When you ask us about online classes, tell us where you are and we will give you the times in your own time zone.

## Common questions

### Are online yoga classes as good as in-person classes?

Live online classes can be very effective, especially for students who already know the basics. In-person classes are better for hands-on correction and for complete beginners. Recorded videos are the weakest option, because nobody can see or correct you.

### Can a complete beginner learn yoga online?

Yes, with care. Start with a few live private sessions so the teacher can check how you move, keep to simple postures at first, and set up your camera so your whole body is visible.

### What do I need for an online yoga class?

A non-slip mat, a clear space the length of the mat plus arm span, a device with a camera placed side-on and low so your whole body is in frame, good sound, and a stable internet connection.

### What time zone are online yoga classes from Kerala in?

Indian Standard Time, UTC+5:30, which does not change during the year. Tell us where you are and we will give you class times in your own time zone.
