Online yoga classes from Kerala: how they work, and who they suit

Live or recorded, group or private, how to set up your space so a teacher can actually see you, and whether a complete beginner can start online.

By Master Aksharanand A S, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 17 September 2026 4 min read

In short

Live online yoga works well if you have a quiet space the length of a mat, a stable connection and a camera that shows your whole body. Beginners can start online, ideally with a few private sessions first so the teacher can check alignment. Online classes suit past students continuing at home, and anyone far from a teacher they trust.

Every season, students leave Fort Kochi at the end of a trip and ask the same question on their last morning: how do I keep this going at home? Others, in Kakkanad or Kottayam or Kuwait, never make it to Fort Kochi at all but want to learn from a traditional teacher. Online classes answer both.

They are not a lesser version of an in-person class, but they are different, and a few simple things decide whether they work. If you are in Fort Kochi and can come in person, our guide to yoga in Fort Kochi has class times.

The difference that matters most is not group versus private, but live versus recorded. A live teacher can see that your knee is drifting inwards. A video cannot.

Can a complete beginner start online?

Yes, with care. The risk for beginners is learning postures slightly wrong, with nobody close enough to notice, and repeating the mistake for months. Three things reduce that risk:

  1. Start with a few live private sessions, so the teacher can see how you move before you join a group.
  2. Keep it simple. Leave headstands and deep backbends until you have practised with a teacher in the room.
  3. Use a camera angle the teacher can actually read — see below.

If you can get to a single in-person class before starting online, even better. The beginner’s guide to your first class covers the basics that apply either way.

Setting up your space

A set-up checklist for online yoga
ItemAim for
SpaceThe length of a mat, plus room to stretch your arms out on both sides
FloorA non-slip mat on a hard, level floor
Camera positionSide-on, a few metres away, low down, in landscape, showing your whole body lying down and standing
LightFacing a window or light source, not with a bright window behind you
SoundA speaker loud enough to hear from the floor, or wireless earphones
ConnectionClose to the router; other devices off the network during class
SurroundingsFurniture moved, pets and children briefed, phone notifications off

Test the camera angle before your first class: lie down on the mat and check that your whole body is in frame. That one check solves most problems.

Getting the most from an online class

  • Keep your camera on. A teacher cannot correct what they cannot see.
  • Send injuries and health details beforehand by message, so the teacher knows before class starts.
  • Log in five minutes early to sort out sound and camera.
  • Stay for the final relaxation. It is the part most often skipped at home, and the part you most need.
  • Practise a little between classes, even ten minutes. That is where online learning really sticks; our 20-minute home routine is a good place to start.

Time zones

Classes from Kerala run on Indian Standard Time, UTC+5:30, and India does not change its clocks. As an example, an evening class at 6:30 pm in India is 2:00 pm in London and 9:00 am in New York during their summer time, and 11:00 pm in Sydney during its winter. When you ask us about online classes, tell us where you are and we will give you the times in your own time zone.

Common questions

Are online yoga classes as good as in-person classes?

Live online classes can be very effective, especially for students who already know the basics. In-person classes are better for hands-on correction and for complete beginners. Recorded videos are the weakest option, because nobody can see or correct you.

Can a complete beginner learn yoga online?

Yes, with care. Start with a few live private sessions so the teacher can check how you move, keep to simple postures at first, and set up your camera so your whole body is visible.

What do I need for an online yoga class?

A non-slip mat, a clear space the length of the mat plus arm span, a device with a camera placed side-on and low so your whole body is in frame, good sound, and a stable internet connection.

What time zone are online yoga classes from Kerala in?

Indian Standard Time, UTC+5:30, which does not change during the year. Tell us where you are and we will give you class times in your own time zone.

How do I join Santhi Yoga’s online classes?

Send us a message on WhatsApp or email with your location, experience and any health considerations. We will reply with current group class times and private session availability and rates.

Can I continue online after practising with you in Fort Kochi?

Yes. Our online classes follow the same traditional approach as our classes in Fort Kochi, so you can continue the practice you started with us from home.
