# Visas for a yoga teacher training in *India*

The visa question worries people more than it should, and catches them out in ways they do not expect. Here is how it works for a month-long course, and what to do early.

By **Master Aksharanand A S**, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 25 September 2026 7 min read

In short

For a 28-day yoga teacher training, most visitors travel on an Indian e-Tourist visa, which allows short courses of study that do not lead to a formal qualification from an Indian university. Apply online at the official government site, indianvisaonline.gov.in, at least two to three weeks before you travel. Longer or academic courses need a student visa applied for at an Indian mission. Rules change, so check the official site for your nationality before you book flights.

Nothing about a visa is interesting until it goes wrong, and then it is the only thing that matters. The good news is that for a four-week training the usual route is simple. The important part is starting early and reading the official site rather than a forum post from 2019.

**One caveat before anything else:** visa rules change, and they differ by nationality. Everything below is a guide to how the system works, not legal advice. Check [indianvisaonline.gov.in](https://indianvisaonline.gov.in/), the official Indian government portal, or your nearest Indian mission, before you book.

## Which visa for a 200-hour course

A 28-day yoga teacher training is a short course that does not award an Indian academic qualification. For most nationalities this sits comfortably inside what a **tourist visa** allows, and it is how the overwhelming majority of trainees travel. Indian tourist visa conditions explicitly contemplate short-term courses of this kind, including yoga.

Three things push you into a different category: a course lasting several months, a course that leads to a formal qualification from a recognised Indian institution, or any paid work. Those need a student visa, or in the last case an employment visa.

## How the e-Tourist visa works

The e-Visa is applied for online, approved by email, and checked at a designated airport on arrival. Cochin International Airport, the one you want for Fort Kochi, is a designated port.

| Point | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **Eligible nationalities** | The e-Visa covers most, but not all, countries. If yours is not listed, you apply at a mission instead. |
| **Duration options** | Different lengths of stay and numbers of entries are offered, with different fees. Choose one that covers your whole trip, not only the course. |
| **Processing time** | Often a few days, but allow two to three weeks. Approvals occasionally take longer with no explanation. |
| **Entry ports** | The e-Visa is valid at designated airports and seaports only. Arriving overland is a different process. |
| **Passport validity** | Six months beyond arrival, with two blank pages, is the usual requirement. |

Apply only through the government portal. The web is full of agent sites with similar names that charge a large fee to fill in the same form, and some are worse than that.

## When you need a student visa

A student visa is the right route if your course runs for several months, if it is delivered by a recognised Indian institution awarding a qualification, or if you are enrolling in a degree or diploma in yoga. It is applied for at an Indian mission with an admission letter from the institution, and it takes longer.

For a single 28-day course at a private school, this is usually unnecessary. If you plan to stay on and train further, it may be worth asking a mission directly.

## Applying, step by step

1. **Check eligibility** for your passport on the official portal.
2. **Have your documents ready:** passport bio page scan, a recent passport photograph on a white background, travel details and an address in India.
3. **Use the school's address** as your address in India, and ask the school for a confirmation letter with the course dates. We send one to every trainee.
4. **Apply at least two to three weeks ahead.** Earlier if you are travelling in high season, December to February.
5. **Pay on the portal** and wait for the approval email.
6. **Print the approval** and carry it. Do not rely on your phone at immigration.

## What to carry

- Printed visa approval and passport.
- Return or onward flight booking.
- The school's confirmation letter, with dates and address.
- Accommodation details for the nights before or after the course.
- Travel insurance that covers the whole stay, including physical activity.

Immigration officers rarely ask for more than the first two, but the letter turns a long conversation into a short one.

## The mistakes that cause trouble

- **Applying through a lookalike agent site** and paying several times the official fee.
- **Cutting it fine.** A three-day gap between applying and flying is a gamble.
- **A visa that ends before your flight home.** Overstaying is treated seriously and fined.
- **Assuming a friend's advice applies.** Conditions differ by nationality and change.
- **Planning to teach or take paid work** on a tourist visa. That is not what it allows.

## Common questions

**Do you need a student visa for a yoga teacher training in India?**

Usually not. A 28-day course at a private yoga school is a short course, and most trainees travel on a tourist or e-Tourist visa. A student visa is for longer study or a qualification from a recognised Indian institution.

**How long does an Indian e-Visa take?**

Often a few days, but allow two to three weeks before your flight. Processing times vary by nationality and season, and occasional applications take longer without explanation.

**Where should you apply for an Indian visa?**

Only on the official government portal, indianvisaonline.gov.in, or at an Indian embassy or consulate. Lookalike agent websites charge much more for the same form.

**Can you fly into Cochin airport on an e-Visa?**

Yes. Cochin International Airport is one of the designated airports for e-Visa arrivals, and it is about 90 minutes by road from Fort Kochi.
