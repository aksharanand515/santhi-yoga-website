Yoga classes in Kochi

What to wear to a yoga class in India, and the etiquette nobody explains

Clothes that work in Kerala’s heat, what to bring and what to leave behind, and the small courtesies that make a class in India go smoothly for you and everyone else in the room.

By Master Aksharanand A S, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 17 September 2026 5 min read

In short

Wear breathable, close-fitting clothes you can bend and turn upside down in, and bring water and a light layer. In India, leave your shoes outside, switch your phone off, arrive a few minutes early, keep strong scents at home, and dress modestly for the walk there. Most schools provide mats.

Clothing questions sound trivial until you are in a forward bend with your T-shirt over your face, or lying in final relaxation shivering because the fan is on and you are soaked. In the heat of Kerala, a few small choices make the difference between a class you enjoy and one you endure.

Etiquette is the other half. Classes in India carry some customs that visitors are rarely told about, and none of them are difficult once you know. For class times and prices in Fort Kochi, see our guide to yoga in Fort Kochi.

Clothes for yoga in Kerala’s heat

Kochi is humid all year, from around 61% in January to 83% in July on average. You will sweat, even in a slow class. So the question is not how to stay dry, but what feels comfortable when you are not.

Fabrics for yoga in a humid climate
FabricIn a Kerala class
CottonCool and comfortable against the skin. Heavier once soaked, but most people prefer it in a slow class.
Linen or cotton blendsVery breathable. Choose ones with a little stretch.
Technical synthetic fabricDries fast and holds its shape. Can feel hot and hold odour in humid air.
Thick or heavy fabricAvoid. It traps heat and restricts movement.

Fit matters more than fabric

  • Tops: fitted, or long enough to tuck in. A loose top will fall over your head in forward bends and inversions.
  • Bottoms: leggings, fitted cotton trousers, or loose trousers gathered at the ankle. Very loose shorts can ride up in seated postures.
  • Avoid zips, large buttons and belts, which dig in when you lie on your front.
  • Tie long hair low, not in a high bun or a clip at the back of the head, so you can lie on your back comfortably.
  • Take off jewellery that swings or catches: large earrings, bracelets, long necklaces.

Dressing for the walk there

Fort Kochi is a relaxed, international neighbourhood, but it is also a place where families live and people go to church, temple and mosque. Covering your shoulders and knees on the street is a simple courtesy, and helpful if you are visiting sights on the same day. Many students simply wear a loose shirt or wrap over their practice clothes and take it off in class.

What to bring, and what to leave behind

A short packing list
BringWhy
WaterFor before and after class. Sip, rather than gulping between postures.
A small towelFor your face and hands, and to keep your grip on the mat.
A light layerFor final relaxation. The body cools quickly once it stops moving, especially under a fan.
A hair tieYou will want it.
Unscented insect repellentFor classes in garden spaces, applied before you arrive.

Leave at home: perfume, strong deodorant or scented oils, which others notice when breathing deeply; valuables; and a mat, unless you prefer your own. Most schools, including ours, provide mats.

Etiquette that matters in India

Shoes off, outside

Just as in Indian homes and temples, shoes come off before you step into the practice space, and are left outside or by the door. Practise barefoot; socks slip on a mat.

Phones off and away

Silent is not enough: a screen lighting up during relaxation disturbs everyone. Switch it off and leave it with your shoes or in your bag.

Arrive early, and if you are late, arrive quietly

Arrive five to ten minutes before the start so you can settle and speak to the teacher. Traditional classes often begin with breathing practice in silence, which is the worst moment to arrive. If you are running late, message the teacher rather than walking in unannounced.

Mind your feet

In India, pointing the soles of your feet at a person, a teacher or an image of a deity is considered disrespectful. If the room has a small altar or pictures of teachers, avoid stretching your feet towards them while sitting.

Chanting: join in, or listen

Many classes open or close with a short chant such as Om. Join in if you like, or sit quietly. Nobody is asking you to adopt a belief.

Hands-on adjustments are your choice

Teachers in traditional classes often correct with a hand. If you would prefer not to be touched, tell the teacher at the start. It is a normal request.

Periods, illness and practice

During your period, whether and how you practise is your choice. Many traditional teachers suggest a gentler practice and avoiding inversions during the first days. You can simply rest in child’s pose when the class goes upside down; no explanation is needed.

If you are ill — a fever, or anything contagious — stay home and come back when you are well. Mention any recent illness to the teacher.

Mistakes visitors often make

  • Wearing a loose vest top that rides up in every forward bend.
  • Eating a full lunch an hour before an afternoon class.
  • Walking in during the opening breathing practice.
  • Leaving a phone on for "just one message".
  • Skipping the final relaxation to beat the crowd out.

Common questions

What should I wear to a yoga class in India?

Breathable, close-fitting clothes you can bend and turn upside down in without adjusting them, such as cotton leggings or trousers and a fitted top. Bring a light layer for the final relaxation and dress modestly for the walk to class.

Can I wear shorts to a yoga class in Kerala?

Fitted shorts are fine in most classes. Very loose shorts ride up in seated postures. For the walk to class, cover your knees and shoulders as a courtesy, especially in residential areas and near places of worship.

Do I need to bring my own yoga mat?

Usually not. Most yoga schools in India provide mats, including ours in Fort Kochi. Bring your own only if you prefer it, and ask ahead if you are unsure.

Should I wear socks to yoga?

No. Practise barefoot. Socks slip on a mat and make standing postures less stable.

Can I do yoga on my period?

Yes, if you feel like it. Many traditional teachers suggest a gentler practice and avoiding inversions in the first days. You can rest in child’s pose whenever you like without explaining.

What if I arrive late to a yoga class?

Message the teacher before you arrive. Traditional classes often begin with quiet breathing, so walking in unannounced during the opening disturbs the room. Aim to arrive five to ten minutes early.
