# Yoga glossary: 106 *Sanskrit* terms explained simply

Sanskrit can make a first yoga class feel like a language lesson. It is less daunting than it sounds: a few dozen words cover almost everything a teacher will say.

By **Master Aksharanand A S**, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 7 October 2026 6 min read

![Teacher training students dressed in white with flower garlands at a traditional ceremony with brass oil lamps](https://santhiyogaindia.com/assets/img/traditional-ceremony-yoga-teacher-training-800.webp)

Sanskrit is still the working language of yoga, in Kerala and everywhere else

In short

This glossary explains 106 Sanskrit words used in yoga classes and teacher trainings, grouped by subject. A few roots unlock most pose names: *asana* means posture, *adho* downward, *urdhva* upward, *ardha* half, *supta* reclining and *parivrtta* revolved. *Pranayama* is breathing practice, *prana* life force, and *savasana* the final relaxation. Philosophy terms such as *ahimsa*, *dharma* and *atman* come from the Yoga Sutras, the Gita and Vedanta.

**Terms**

106

**Most useful word**

*Asana*, posture

**Unlocks pose names**

Adho, urdhva, ardha, supta, parivrtta

**Breath**

Puraka in, rechaka out, kumbhaka hold

**Ethics**

Five yamas, five niyamas

**Pronunciation**

Long vowels, soft “v”, aspirated “th”

Sanskrit is the classical language of India, and the language of the texts yoga comes from. Most teachers, including us, use the Sanskrit name alongside the English one. You do not need to learn it to practise. It does help to know what you are hearing. If you are new, start with our [guide to your first yoga class](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/first-yoga-class-beginners-guide/), then keep this page open in another tab.

Spellings vary, because Sanskrit is written in Devanagari script and there are several ways to bring it into English letters. *Sirsasana* and *Shirshasana* are the same pose. We use the spellings most common in classes.

## What do the basic yoga terms mean?

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Yoga** | From *yuj*, to join or yoke. Both the practice and its goal: a mind brought to stillness, or union with the true Self. |
| **Hatha** | The branch of yoga that works through the body and breath: postures, breathing, cleansing and locks. Often read as *ha* (sun) and *tha* (moon). See our [guide to Hatha Yoga](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/hatha-yoga-explained/). |
| **Asana** | A posture. Originally “seat”: in the Yoga Sutras, a steady, comfortable sitting position. |
| **Pranayama** | Breathing practice. *Prana* plus *ayama*, extension or control. |
| **Prana** | Life force or vital energy, carried in the breath. See [the five vayus](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/prana-vayus-explained/). |
| **Nadi** | A channel through which prana flows. Texts speak of 72,000. |
| **Ida** | The left, cooling, “lunar” nadi, associated with the left nostril. |
| **Pingala** | The right, heating, “solar” nadi, associated with the right nostril. |
| **Sushumna** | The central channel, running along the spine. |
| **Chakra** | “Wheel”. An energy centre along the sushumna, traditionally seven. See [the chakras explained](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/chakras-explained/). |
| **Kundalini** | Dormant spiritual energy said to rest at the base of the spine. |
| **Bandha** | A lock: a muscular contraction that directs prana. See [the three bandhas](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/bandhas-explained/). |
| **Mudra** | A seal or gesture, usually of the hands, sometimes of the whole body. See [mudras explained](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/mudras-explained/). |
| **Drishti** | A gazing point, used to steady attention in a pose. |
| **Vinyasa** | Placing in a special way: moving between poses linked to the breath. Also a style of class. |
| **Sadhana** | Daily spiritual practice, kept up over time. |
| **Abhyasa** | Steady, repeated practice. One of the two keys to yoga in the Yoga Sutras. |
| **Vairagya** | Non-attachment, the other key. Practice without clinging to results. |
| **Shatkarma** | The six cleansing practices of Hatha Yoga, such as neti. See [jal neti and the shatkarmas](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/jal-neti-and-the-shatkarmas/). |
| **Kriya** | An action or technique, especially a cleansing or energetic one. |

## How do you pronounce Sanskrit yoga words?

A few rules get you most of the way:

- **Vowels have a short and a long form.** The short *a* is like the “u” in “but”; the long *ā* is like the “a” in “father”. *Āsana* starts long: “aa-suh-nuh”, not “a-sah-na”.
- **“Th” is not the English “th”.** It is a “t” with a breath after it, as in “hot-house”. *Hatha* is closer to “hut-ha” than to “hath-a”.
- **“Ph” is not “f”.** It is a “p” with a breath after it.
- **“V” is soft,** somewhere between an English v and w.
- **“Ch” is as in “church”,** so *chakra* starts like “chuck”.
- **“Sh” and “s” are distinct**, but if you mix them up nobody will mind.
- **A final short “a” is light.** In Hindi it is often dropped altogether, which is why you will hear *yoga* said as “yog” in north India.

## What is the easiest way to learn yoga Sanskrit?

1. **Learn the roots, not the names.** Once you know *adho*, *urdhva*, *mukha* and *svana*, you know two poses without memorising either.
2. **Learn ten poses at a time,** starting with the ones you practise most. The [12 basic Sivananda postures](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/sivananda-12-basic-postures/) make a good first list.
3. **Say them aloud** as you practise. Hearing yourself fixes them in memory.
4. **Learn the meaning with the word.** *Savasana* means corpse pose, and knowing that tells you what to do in it.
5. **Chant.** Chanting is how Sanskrit has always been learned. A few lines sung daily teach rhythm and pronunciation faster than any list.

## Common questions

**What does asana mean?**

Asana means posture. In Patanjali’s Yoga Sutras it originally meant a steady, comfortable seat for meditation; it later came to mean any yoga posture.

**What does namaste mean?**

Namaste is a respectful greeting meaning “I bow to you”, usually said with the palms together at the chest. It is used every day in India, not only in yoga.

**What is the difference between yama and niyama?**

The yamas are five ethical restraints in how we treat others: non-harming, truthfulness, non-stealing, moderation and non-possessiveness. The niyamas are five personal observances: cleanliness, contentment, discipline, self-study and surrender.

**Why do yoga teachers use Sanskrit names?**

Sanskrit is the language of the original yoga texts, and the names often describe what the pose is, which English names do not always capture. It also gives teachers from different countries a shared vocabulary.

**How do you pronounce Hatha?**

Close to “hut-ha”. The “th” in Sanskrit is a “t” followed by a breath, not the English “th” in “path”.

**Do I need to learn Sanskrit to do yoga?**

No. Most teachers give the English name as well, and you can practise for years without learning any Sanskrit. Knowing the common roots simply makes classes easier to follow.
