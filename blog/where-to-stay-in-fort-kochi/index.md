# Where to stay in *Fort Kochi*

Kochi is three quite different places wearing one name, and booking sites are not always clear about which one you are looking at.

By **Master Aksharanand A S**, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 24 September 2026 6 min read

![The Chinese fishing nets on the Fort Kochi waterfront](https://santhiyogaindia.com/assets/img/chinese-fishing-nets-fort-kochi-800.webp)

Most of what visitors come for is within a walk of the waterfront

In short

Stay in Fort Kochi itself if you want the old town, the waterfront and a walkable base — it suits most visitors. Mattancherry, next door, is quieter, a little cheaper and closer to the spice warehouses and synagogue. Ernakulam, across the harbour, is the modern city: better for shopping, transport and late flights, but a ferry or long drive from everything visitors come for. Homestays are the characteristic option and usually the best value.

We are asked this most weeks, usually by someone who has already booked and is discovering that "Kochi" covers a lot of ground.

## Three places, one name

Kochi (Cochin) spreads across a peninsula, a set of islands and a mainland city. For a visitor, three parts matter.

**Fort Kochi** is the old colonial town at the tip of the peninsula: the fishing nets, the lanes of lime-washed houses, the churches, the cafés. Flat, compact and walkable.

**Mattancherry** adjoins it to the south — the Dutch Palace, the synagogue, Jew Town's spice warehouses and antique shops. A twenty-minute walk or a five-minute auto ride from the Fort Kochi waterfront.

**Ernakulam** is the mainland city across the harbour: malls, hospitals, the railway stations, most of the population. It is a fifteen-minute ferry or a forty-five-minute drive round, and it is where people accidentally book when the listing simply says Kochi.

## Fort Kochi itself

**Suits:** most first-time visitors, anyone here for two or three nights, anyone who would rather walk than arrange transport.

You can reach the waterfront, the sunset, dinner, a Kathakali performance and the lanes on foot. Mornings are quiet and the light is lovely. It is the most expensive of the three, and in December and January it is busy.

Within Fort Kochi, closer to the waterfront and Princess Street means more convenient and a little noisier; the residential lanes inland — Chirattapalam and around — are quieter and still a short walk from everything. That is where the school is, and where a lot of the nicer homestays are.

## Mattancherry

**Suits:** a second visit, longer stays, anyone who wants more life and less tourism per square metre.

Noticeably cheaper for the same standard, and a working neighbourhood rather than a visited one — spice godowns, workshops, a lot of ordinary daily business. You will use autos in the evening rather than walking back, which is a minor cost and easy to arrange.

## Ernakulam

**Suits:** very early or very late flights, business trips, shopping, or if you need a hospital.

Better transport links and generally better value, but you are a ferry or a long drive from the old town, and the last ferries are earlier than most people expect. If your reason for coming is Fort Kochi, staying here means committing to the crossing twice a day.

One exception worth knowing: if you land after midnight or fly out before dawn, a night near the airport at Nedumbassery makes more sense than either. The airport is about 45 km north of Fort Kochi — details in [getting from the airport](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/kochi-airport-to-fort-kochi/).

## Kinds of place to stay

**Homestays** are the characteristic Fort Kochi option and often the best value: a few rooms in a family house, breakfast included, and hosts who will arrange a driver or tell you which fish is good today. Quality varies enormously between neighbours, so read recent reviews rather than relying on the category.

**Heritage hotels** occupy restored Portuguese and Dutch merchant houses — courtyards, antique furniture, high ceilings. Atmospheric and comfortable at a higher price. Check for air conditioning if you are coming between March and May.

**Hostels** have grown in number and are clustered near the waterfront and Princess Street.

**Resorts** sit mostly on Willingdon Island, Bolgatty and Vypin — good if you want a pool and quiet, less good if you want to walk to dinner.

## What to check before booking

- **Which Kochi.** Look at the map pin, not the name. This is the mistake that costs people a day.
- **Air conditioning and a fan** if you are coming March to May. Humidity matters more than temperature here.
- **Hot water,** which is not universal in cheaper rooms and is sometimes on a timer.
- **Noise.** Rooms facing Princess Street or a church are livelier than the listing suggests. Kerala has festivals with early-morning loudspeakers.
- **Mosquito screens or a net**, particularly near the water and in the monsoon.
- **Airport transfer,** which most homestays will arrange more cheaply and reliably than an app at 2am.
- **How long you are staying.** Many homestays discount weekly stays if you ask directly rather than booking through a platform.

## If you are here to practise

Stay in Fort Kochi, and ideally in the lanes around Chirattapalam rather than out on the ring road. A 7:30 class is a pleasure when it is a seven-minute walk and a chore when it is a twenty-minute auto negotiation in the dark.

Anywhere in Fort Kochi proper is close enough. Mattancherry works for the afternoon class and is a slightly early start for the morning one. Ernakulam does not really work for a daily practice.

## Common questions

**Is it better to stay in Fort Kochi or Ernakulam?**

Fort Kochi, for almost every visitor. It is where the old town, the waterfront and the restaurants are, and it is walkable. Ernakulam is the modern city across the harbour — better for transport, shopping and late flights, but a ferry or a long drive from what most people come to see.

**How many nights should I spend in Fort Kochi?**

Two or three for the sights at a reasonable pace. A week if you are building a daily yoga practice or simply want to stop moving, which is what the town is best at.

**Are homestays in Fort Kochi good?**

Generally yes, and they are often the best value — a few rooms in a family house with breakfast and genuinely useful local advice. Standards vary a lot between neighbouring houses, so read recent reviews rather than trusting the category.

**Where should I stay to be near yoga classes?**

The residential lanes of Fort Kochi, around Chirattapalam and inland from the waterfront. Anywhere in Fort Kochi proper is a walk from a 7:30 class. Ernakulam is too far for a daily practice.
