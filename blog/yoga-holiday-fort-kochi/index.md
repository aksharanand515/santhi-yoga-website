## Why a week changes things

- **Days 1–2: learning the shape.** You learn the order of the class — opening relaxation, breathing, sun salutations, postures, deep relaxation — and stop wondering what comes next.
- **Days 3–5: corrections build.** The same teacher sees the same body every day, so yesterday’s correction becomes today’s starting point.
- **Days 6–7 and beyond: you can practise alone.** You leave with a sequence you know well enough to keep going at home.

That is the reason a week of consecutive classes teaches more than a month of occasional ones, and the reason we offer 30% off to students who practise with us for more than a week.

One two-hour class a day is plenty for most people, especially at first. You are welcome at both, but a rested body learns more than a tired one.

## What it costs

- **Classes:** ₹500 each (a limited-time offer; normally ₹600, and ₹700 from November 2026). If you practise with us for more than a week, classes are 30% off — ask us how it works for your dates when you message.
- **A private session**, if you want one mid-week: ₹1,500, rising to ₹2,000 in November 2026.
- **Getting around:** Fort Kochi is walkable; the Water Metro to Ernakulam takes about 20 minutes, with fares from around ₹20.
- **Accommodation and food:** these vary widely by season and taste, from simple homestays to heritage hotels.

## Where to stay

Choose somewhere within walking distance of Chirattapalam, so a 7:30 am class does not mean an early taxi. Fort Kochi has everything from family homestays to restored heritage buildings. Daily classes do not include accommodation; if you want everything arranged for you, our [28-day teacher training](https://santhiyogaindia.com/teacher-training/) includes accommodation and three meals a day.

## Eating around classes

- **Before a morning class:** nothing, or a little fruit.
- **Before the afternoon class:** an early, light lunch — about three hours after a full meal.
- **Afterwards:** Kerala’s vegetarian food suits a yoga week well, and the town has plenty of it.

## When to come

**November to February** is dry, comfortable and the busiest time. **March to May** is hot, and the afternoon class often suits bodies better. **June to September** is the monsoon: humid, green and quiet. Our classes run every day, so pick the season that suits you. More on timing in [morning or evening yoga](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/best-time-to-do-yoga/).

If you would rather have the week arranged for you, our retreat project [Samya Retreats](https://samyaretreats.com/retreat.html) runs seven-day retreats across Fort Kochi and Munnar on four 2027 dates: 13–19 March, 4–10 September, 10–16 October and 4–10 December.

## Common questions

### How long should a yoga holiday in Fort Kochi be?

At least a week, with a class every day. That is long enough for the class to become familiar and for corrections from the same teacher to build on each other, and it qualifies for 30% off at Santhi Yoga.

### Is there a discount for a week of yoga classes in Fort Kochi?

At Santhi Yoga, students who practise for more than a week get 30% off classes. Mention it when you message the school on WhatsApp at +91 7907714144.

### Can I do two yoga classes a day?

You are welcome at both the morning and afternoon class. Most people find one two-hour class a day is plenty, especially at first, and practise better rested.

### Does Santhi Yoga provide accommodation?

Not with daily classes; stay anywhere in Fort Kochi within walking distance of Chirattapalam. Accommodation and meals are included in the 28-day teacher training.

### What is the best time of year for a yoga holiday in Kerala?

November to February has the most comfortable weather. The monsoon, June to September, is wet but quiet and green, and daily classes continue.

### What should I pack for a yoga week in Fort Kochi?

Breathable cotton clothes you can bend in, a light layer for relaxation, a water bottle, and modest clothing for sightseeing. Mats are provided at Santhi Yoga.
