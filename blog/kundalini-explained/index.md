# What is kundalini? *An honest explanation*

No word in yoga attracts more excitement, or more nonsense. The traditional teaching is quieter than the internet suggests, and more sensible.

By **Master Aksharanand A S**, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 25 October 2026 5 min read

![The garden yoga pavilion of Santhi School of Yoga in Fort Kochi in morning light](https://santhiyogaindia.com/assets/img/yoga-pavilion-garden-fort-kochi-800.webp)

In the tradition, kundalini is approached slowly, through a steady daily practice.

In short

**Kundalini, "the coiled one", is described in the Hatha Yoga texts as a dormant energy resting at the base of the spine. Through pranayama, bandhas, mudras and meditation it is said to rise through the central channel, sushumna, and the chakras, bringing deep states of meditation.** It is a traditional model of inner experience, not a physical force. Kundalini Yoga, the modern style taught worldwide, is a separate system founded in 1969.

Students ask me about kundalini more than almost anything else in the philosophy classes, usually with a mixture of curiosity and alarm. Both are understandable. Here is what the tradition actually says, and how we approach it in a Hatha school.

## What the word means

*Kundalini* comes from *kundala*, a coil or ring. In the texts she is pictured as a serpent coiled three and a half times at the base of the spine, asleep, with her mouth closing the entrance to the central channel.

The *Hatha Yoga Pradipika* gives a whole chapter to her. It calls her the support of all yoga practice and says that when she wakes, the knots of the subtle body are pierced and the breath moves through [sushumna, the central nadi](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/nadis-ida-pingala-sushumna/).

## Where it sits in the yogic map

Kundalini is the last piece of a larger picture. Prana moves in [five directions](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/prana-vayus-explained/). It flows through thousands of nadis, of which ida, pingala and sushumna matter most. Along sushumna lie [the seven chakras](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/chakras-explained/). Kundalini is the energy that, in this model, rises from the lowest chakra to the crown.

Read this way, kundalini is not an extra ingredient. It describes what happens when the rest of the practice is done well over a long time: attention stops scattering, the breath becomes even, and the mind settles in the centre.

## How the texts say it is awakened

The classical methods are not dramatic. They are the ordinary tools of Hatha Yoga, practised for years:

- **Asana**, to make the body steady enough to sit still.
- **Pranayama**, especially [nadi shodhana](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/nadi-shodhana-alternate-nostril-breathing/) to balance the side channels, and breath retention.
- **Bandhas**, [the three locks](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/bandhas-explained/), said to direct prana into the central channel.
- **Mudras**, particularly the advanced ones described in the Hatha texts.
- **Meditation**, and above all [a steady ethical life](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/yamas-and-niyamas/), which the texts treat as the foundation.

Notice what is missing: weekend shortcuts. Every traditional source assumes a teacher, a stable daily practice and a great deal of patience.

## Kundalini the energy versus Kundalini Yoga the style

|  | Kundalini in Hatha Yoga | Kundalini Yoga (style) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | A concept within classical Hatha and Tantra | A branded system of classes and teacher trainings |
| Source | Hatha Yoga Pradipika, Gheranda Samhita, Tantric texts | Yogi Bhajan, from 1969 in the United States |
| Typical practice | Asana, pranayama, bandhas, meditation over years | Kriyas: set sequences of movement, breath of fire, chanting and meditation |
| Clothing | Whatever is comfortable | Often white, with head coverings |

Many people who search for kundalini are really looking for the style. It is a legitimate practice and plenty of people love it. It is simply a different thing from what the Hatha texts mean by the word, and it is not what we teach.

## What is a kundalini awakening?

In the traditional sense, it is the rising of this energy into the higher chakras, experienced as profound stillness, clarity and a sense of unity. The texts describe it as the culmination of practice, not an event that happens to beginners.

In modern usage the phrase is used much more loosely, for intense experiences of heat, tingling, emotion or involuntary movement during practice or meditation. Those experiences are real and worth taking seriously. They are also often the result of forcing practices, especially long breath retentions and rapid breathing, beyond what the body and nervous system are ready for.

## A word on safety

The old texts warn repeatedly against forcing the breath, and they are right to. If intense practices leave you anxious, sleepless, dizzy or disturbed, stop them, return to gentle asana and slow, even breathing, and talk to an experienced teacher. If symptoms persist, see a doctor; there is no shame in it and it is the sensible thing to do.

People with a history of psychosis, severe anxiety, epilepsy or heart conditions should avoid strong pranayama such as [kapalabhati](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/kapalabhati-breathing/) and long retentions unless a qualified teacher and their doctor agree.

## Where to learn it in India

Rishikesh has the largest number of schools advertising kundalini courses; Kerala and the south lean more towards classical Hatha. Whatever you choose, ask who the teacher trained with, how many years they have practised, and how they handle students who have a difficult experience. Our [red flags guide](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/yoga-teacher-training-red-flags/) applies here too.

At our school kundalini is taught as philosophy, within a classical Hatha practice: what the texts say, why the practices are sequenced as they are, and why patience is the method. ## Common questions

**What is kundalini in simple words?**

In the Hatha Yoga texts, kundalini is a dormant energy pictured as a coiled serpent at the base of the spine. Long, steady practice is said to awaken it and draw it up the central channel, bringing deep meditation.

**Is kundalini dangerous?**

The concept is not dangerous, but forcing intense breathing and retention practices can be destabilising for some people. Practise gradually, with a teacher, and stop if you feel anxious, dizzy or unable to sleep.

**Is Kundalini Yoga the same as Hatha Yoga?**

No. Kundalini Yoga is a modern style introduced by Yogi Bhajan in 1969, built on kriyas, breath of fire and chanting. Hatha Yoga is the older classical system whose texts first describe kundalini.

**How long does it take to awaken kundalini?**

Traditional sources speak of years of steady practice under a teacher, and describe it as the fruit of the whole path rather than a goal to rush towards.

**Can I learn kundalini yoga in Kerala?**

Kerala schools mostly teach classical Hatha Yoga, where kundalini is studied as part of philosophy and pranayama. For the Kundalini Yoga style, Rishikesh has more dedicated schools.
