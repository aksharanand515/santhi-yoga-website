# Yoga teacher training in Kerala: *how to choose a school*

Kerala is one of the best places in India to train as a yoga teacher, and one of the most confusing to shop for. The region you choose shapes the month as much as the school does.

By **Master Aksharanand A S**, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 25 October 2026 5 min read

![Master Aksharanand giving a hands-on backbend adjustment to a trainee in the garden pavilion](https://santhiyogaindia.com/assets/img/hands-on-backbend-adjustment-garden-pavilion-800.webp)

A training in Kerala is a month of two practices a day in the heat. Choose the setting with care.

In short

**Most 200-hour yoga teacher trainings in Kerala run 21–28 days and cost roughly US$900–1,600 including shared accommodation and vegetarian meals. The main places to train are Varkala (beach, many schools), Fort Kochi (historic town, small schools), Kovalam (beach and Ayurveda), Neyyar Dam (the Sivananda ashram) and the hills around Munnar.** November to March is the most comfortable season. Choose on the lead teacher, the batch size and the daily timetable before the location.

I have taught yoga in Fort Kochi for over fifteen years, and students who write to us have usually looked at three or four other Kerala schools first. This is the comparison I would give a friend. It sits alongside our broader guides to [choosing a 200-hour yoga teacher training](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/how-to-choose-yoga-teacher-training/) and [Kerala versus Rishikesh](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/kerala-vs-rishikesh-yoga-teacher-training/).

## Why train in Kerala?

- **Tradition.** Kerala has a strong classical Hatha culture and the long-established Sivananda ashram at Neyyar Dam.
- **Ayurveda.** The state is the centre of Ayurvedic medicine in India, and many courses include an introduction to it. See [how Ayurveda and yoga fit together](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/ayurveda-and-yoga-in-kerala/).
- **Food.** Vegetarian South Indian cooking is the norm, not a special request; see [sattvic food explained](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/sattvic-food-explained/).
- **Climate.** Warm all year, which makes the body supple from the first morning. It also means heat and humidity to plan for.
- **Ease.** Kochi and Thiruvananthapuram both have international airports, and Kerala is among the safest and most literate states in India.

## Where to train in Kerala

| Place | Setting | Typical schools | Suits you if |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Varkala | Clifftop beach town, about 50 km north of Thiruvananthapuram | Many international schools, often Yoga Alliance registered, often larger batches | You want the beach, a social scene and plenty of choice |
| Fort Kochi | Historic port town in central Kerala, near Kochi airport | A few small, long-established local schools | You want culture, a real town and small groups |
| Kovalam | Beach resort south of Thiruvananthapuram | Resort and Ayurveda-centre trainings | You want Ayurvedic treatment alongside training |
| Neyyar Dam | Forested lakeside ashram near Thiruvananthapuram | The Sivananda Yoga Vedanta Dhanwantari Ashram | You want a strict ashram routine and lineage |
| Munnar and the hills | Tea estates and hills, cooler nights | Occasional retreat-style trainings | You find coastal heat hard and want quiet |

## When to go

**November to March** is the most comfortable: dry, sunny, with cooler mornings. It is also the busiest, so popular schools fill early.

**April and May** are hot and humid; training is very possible, just slower. **June to September** is the monsoon: green, cool and quiet, with heavy rain. Some beach schools close for it, while town schools like ours carry on. Our month-by-month page on [the best time to visit Fort Kochi](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/best-time-to-visit-fort-kochi/) shows what each season is like.

## Questions to ask any Kerala school

1. **Who teaches the course, and for how many of the hours?** A famous founder who appears twice is not the same as one who teaches every day.
2. **How many students are in a batch?** Hands-on correction becomes hard above about fifteen.
3. **What does a day look like, hour by hour?** Ask for the real timetable.
4. **What is the certificate, exactly?** Yoga Alliance RYS, a YCB certification, or the school’s own? See [AYUSH YCB versus Yoga Alliance](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/ycb-vs-yoga-alliance/).
5. **What is excluded?** Airport transfers, laundry, private rooms, excursions, Ayurvedic treatments.
6. **What happens if I have to cancel?** Get the refund policy in writing.
7. **Can I speak to a past student?** A good school will introduce you.

If an answer is vague, or the school pushes you to pay quickly, read our [red flags guide](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/yoga-teacher-training-red-flags/) before going further.

## Common questions

**How much does yoga teacher training cost in Kerala?**

A residential 200-hour course in Kerala usually costs about US$900 to 1,600 with shared accommodation and meals. Resort and Ayurveda-centre courses can reach US$3,000.

**Which is the best place in Kerala for yoga teacher training?**

It depends on what you want: Varkala for the beach and choice of schools, Fort Kochi for culture and small groups, Neyyar Dam for an ashram, and Kovalam for Ayurveda.

**How long is a yoga teacher training in Kerala?**

Most 200-hour courses run 21 to 28 days, six days a week, with one free day each week.

**What is the best month for yoga teacher training in Kerala?**

November to March is the most comfortable. April and May are hot, and June to September is the monsoon, when some beach schools close.

**Is Kerala better than Rishikesh for yoga teacher training?**

Neither is better overall. Kerala is warmer, greener and quieter, with strong Ayurveda and Hatha traditions; Rishikesh has more schools and a Himalayan setting.
