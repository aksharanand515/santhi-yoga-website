# Yoga for weight loss: *an honest answer*

A great deal of what is written on this subject is designed to sell a course. Here is what we actually see in people who practise every day for a month, which is a slightly different picture.

By **Master Aksharanand A S**, founder of Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies Fort Kochi, Kerala Updated 24 September 2026 6 min read

![A student practising an arm balance outdoors during a retreat](https://santhiyogaindia.com/assets/img/handstand-practice-yoga-retreat-800.webp)

Strength changes shape long before the scales move

In short

Yoga on its own burns relatively few calories — a traditional Hatha class is closer to a brisk walk than a run — so it is a poor tool for weight loss if that is all you change. Where it genuinely helps is indirect: it lowers stress eating, improves sleep, builds the daily habit that other exercise needs, and makes people notice what and how they eat. Used alongside walking and a sensible diet it works well. Used instead of them it usually does not.

People ask this often enough that it deserves a straight answer rather than a hopeful one.

## The calorie question

A steady traditional Hatha class — the kind taught here, with long holds, breathing practice and a twenty-minute relaxation — is not a high-calorie activity. Over two hours it sits somewhere near a long walk. A fast vinyasa or hot class is higher, roughly comparable to a moderate cycle, but still well below running.

So if you take up yoga, change nothing else, and expect the scales to move on the strength of energy burned, you will probably be disappointed. Anyone telling you otherwise is selling something.

That is the honest version. It is also not the whole picture, because weight is not only arithmetic.

## Where yoga actually helps

The effects we see are indirect, and in practice they matter more than the calorie count.

**It interrupts stress eating.** A great deal of unwanted eating is not hunger. It is a way of handling a state — tired, agitated, bored at nine in the evening. Practices that settle the nervous system take the edge off that state directly, which is why the same tools that help [anxiety](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/yoga-for-anxiety-and-stress/) tend to help here.

**It fixes sleep.** Short sleep reliably increases appetite and makes high-calorie food more appealing. Improving sleep is one of the most consistent effects of a daily practice, and it does more for eating than any amount of willpower. There is a full [evening sequence here](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/yoga-for-better-sleep/).

**It builds the habit that other exercise needs.** This is underrated. People who establish a daily twenty minutes on a mat find it far easier to add a walk or a swim, because the hard part — showing up every day — is already solved.

**It makes you notice.** Spending an hour paying close attention to your body has a way of carrying over to the table. Students on month-long courses eat [sattvic vegetarian food](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/sattvic-food-explained/) at fixed times, and most report that the rhythm, not the menu, is what changed their eating when they went home.

**It changes shape before it changes weight.** Holding postures builds real strength through the back, shoulders and core. People often find clothes fitting differently while the scales are unmoved, which is a genuine result even though it does not look like one.

## Which styles burn more

If energy expenditure is the goal, more vigorous styles do more of it: ashtanga, power yoga and faster vinyasa classes. Hot yoga feels harder mainly because of the heat rather than the work, and the immediate weight change after a hot class is water, not fat.

But there is a trade-off worth naming. The vigorous styles are harder to sustain daily, carry more injury risk for beginners, and drop the parts — breathing, long relaxation — that produce the indirect benefits above. A slower practice you do every day beats a hard one you do twice a month. [The styles compared](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/types-of-yoga-classes/).

## How to practise for it

- **Daily and short** beats occasional and long. Twenty minutes most days.
- **Include sun salutations.** [Surya namaskar](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/surya-namaskar-steps/) is the one traditional sequence that reliably raises the heart rate. Ten to twelve rounds at a steady pace is a real warm-up and a real effort.
- **Hold the standing postures.** Warrior and chair held for five breaths do more than passing through them.
- **Add walking.** The single most effective thing most people can add, and it costs nothing.
- **Keep the relaxation.** It is tempting to cut it to save time. It is doing more than the postures for stress and sleep.

## The part nobody wants to hear

Weight is determined mostly by what and how much you eat. No practice, however diligent, outruns that — and it is not a failure of the practice that this is so.

The useful yogic contribution is not a diet plan. It is a set of habits that make eating well easier: eat at regular times, make the midday meal the main one, stop before you are full, sit down without a screen, and leave a couple of hours between eating and practising. These are old instructions and they hold up well.

We are yoga teachers, not dietitians. If you have significant weight to lose, a thyroid condition, diabetes or an eating disorder, work with a doctor or a registered dietitian. Yoga sits alongside that.

## What to expect, honestly

In our experience of month-long courses, where people practise twice daily and eat simply on a fixed schedule: most lose some weight, nearly all report better sleep and digestion, and the changes people talk about a year later are almost never about the scales. They are about having a thing they do every morning.

## Common questions

**Can you lose weight with yoga alone?**

Usually not much, if nothing else changes. A traditional Hatha class burns roughly what a long walk burns. Yoga helps weight more through better sleep, less stress eating and a daily habit than through calories burned.

**Which yoga is best for weight loss?**

More vigorous styles like ashtanga, power yoga or faster vinyasa expend more energy. But a slower practice you sustain daily generally does more overall than a demanding one you manage occasionally.

**How many times a week should I do yoga to lose weight?**

Most days, for twenty minutes or more, rather than two long sessions. Add sun salutations for the cardiovascular part, and add walking, which is the most effective thing most people can bolt on.

**Does hot yoga burn more fat?**

It feels harder mostly because of the heat rather than the work, and the immediate drop on the scales afterwards is water you will replace by drinking. It is not notably better than any other vigorous style for fat loss.
