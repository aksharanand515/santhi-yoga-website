This manual has three sections. **Section I** covers the philosophy, history and inner practices of yoga. **Section II**, the largest, covers the body and the practices that work through it: anatomy, biomechanics, the twelve basic postures, safety, prāṇāyāma and meditation. **Section III** covers the craft and ethics of teaching, and ends with the practicum and final examination. Appendices provide glossaries, indexes, a cue library, sample classes, assessment forms and the full reference list.

Every chapter opens with **learning objectives** and closes with a **chapter review**: key takeaways, reflection questions, teacher-practice exercises, a knowledge check (answers are printed upside down beneath it) and a teaching assignment. The assignments build towards your practicum, so do them in order.

## Reading the evidence labels {.nonum}

Yoga is an ancient tradition studied today by historians, philosophers, physiologists and clinicians — and each speaks with a different kind of authority. To keep them from blurring, statements that could be misread are tagged:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| [[H]] | **Historical evidence** — what texts, inscriptions and archaeology show about when and where something appeared. |
| [[T]] | **Traditional teaching** — what the yoga tradition teaches, as the tradition teaches it. It is presented faithfully and with respect; it is not a scientific claim. |
| [[I]] | **Interpretation** — a philosophical or teacher’s reading of a text or practice; reasonable people differ. |
| [[S]] | **Modern scientific understanding** — what current research suggests, with a citation and an honest statement of its strength. |
| [[C]] | **Clinical caution** — a safety point where expert consensus, physiology or case reports advise care. |

A traditional teaching is not “wrong” because science has not measured it, and a scientific finding is not “spiritual” because it concerns yoga. We ask you to hold both honestly — and to teach your students to do the same.

## The boxes {.nonum}

::: keyconcept
Key concepts summarise an idea you must be able to explain to a student in your own words.
:::

::: tip
Teacher tips pass on practical wisdom from the teaching hall — what works, what to watch for, what to say.
:::

::: safety
Safety boxes flag contraindications, precautions and moments when a student should stop or be referred on.
:::

::: exercise
Practical exercises are done on the mat or with a partner during the course.
:::

::: evidence
Evidence boxes summarise what research does and does not show, with citations.
:::

::: trad
Traditional teaching boxes present a teaching as the tradition gives it.
:::

## Citations {.nonum}

Sources are cited as (Author, year) and listed in full in @ch:references. Yoga texts are cited by chapter and verse — for example YS 2.46 for *Yoga Sūtra* book 2, sūtra 46, and HYP 1.17 for *Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā* chapter 1, verse 17. Verse numbering of the *Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā* differs slightly between editions; this manual follows @akers2002.
