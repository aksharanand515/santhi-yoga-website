A short list for continuing study, chosen for reliability and usefulness to a new teacher. Full details of these and all other works cited are in the References. Start with one book from each group; return to the primary texts throughout your life.

## Primary texts in translation {.nonum}

- **Yoga Sūtra.** @bryant2009 — a scholarly translation with extensive traditional commentary; @satchidananda1978 — a practical, accessible commentary from within Swami Sivananda’s lineage.
- **Bhagavad Gītā.** @easwaran2007 for readability; @sargeant2009 for word-by-word Sanskrit.
- **Upaniṣads.** @olivelle1996 — an accurate, annotated translation of the principal Upaniṣads.
- **Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā.** @muktibodhananda1998, with practical commentary; @akers2002 for a plain translation.
- **Vedānta.** Śaṅkara’s *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi* [@madhavananda1921].

## The Sivananda tradition {.nonum}

- @vishnudevananda1960 — the classic manual of the twelve basic postures and the five points of yoga.
- @lidell1983 — the illustrated companion to Sivananda practice.
- @sivananda1935 and @vishnudevananda1978 — prāṇāyāma, meditation and mantra in the tradition’s own words.

## Other lineages {.nonum}

- @iyengar1966 and @iyengar1981 — detailed technique of posture and breath from a closely related modern lineage.
- @satyananda1969 — the Bihar School’s comprehensive practice manual, including bandhas and mudrās.
- @desikachar1995 — adapting practice to the individual, from the Krishnamacharya tradition.

## History and scholarship {.nonum}

- @mallinson2017 — primary sources on haṭha and early yoga, translated and introduced; the best single book for understanding where the practices come from.
- @singleton2010 and @demichelis2004 — how modern posture practice took shape.
- @white2014 — the life of the Yoga Sūtra through history.
- @flood1996 — the religious and cultural context.
- @feuerstein2008 — a broad survey of the tradition; readable, though some of its historical claims have been revised by later scholarship.

## Anatomy, physiology and movement {.nonum}

- @kaminoff2021 — clear, yoga-specific anatomy with excellent illustrations.
- @neumann2017 — the standard kinesiology text, for depth.
- @calais2006 — the anatomy of breathing for teachers.
- @clark2016 — individual variation in the skeleton and why poses look different in different bodies.
- @coulter2001 and @keil2014 — further yoga-specific anatomy and kinesiology.
- @kendall2005 — muscle testing and posture, for the detail of individual muscles.
- @butler2013 — a readable introduction to modern pain science.

## Meditation and the mind {.nonum}

- @gunaratana2011 and @hanh1975 — practical guides to mindfulness.
- @kabatzinn1990 — the foundation of mindfulness-based stress reduction.

## Teaching {.nonum}

- @farhi2006 — the teacher–student relationship and ethics.
- @stephens2010 — practical teaching methods.
- @emerson2011 — trauma-sensitive yoga; @vanderkolk2014b for the wider context of trauma and the body, read with awareness that some of its claims are debated.
- @remski2019 — a sobering account of abuse in yoga communities, and how to prevent it.

## Evidence and health {.nonum}

- @khalsa2016 — yoga in health care: research, principles and practice.
- @broad2012 — a journalist’s account of the risks and benefits; read critically, alongside the research reviews cited in @ch:safety.
