::: objectives
By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

- plan your continuing professional development after this course;
- describe the practical foundations of a teaching career: credentials, insurance, first aid, record-keeping and legal compliance;
- market your teaching honestly and ethically;
- design a sustainable working life that protects your practice, body, voice and finances.

## After the 200 hours

Completing a 200-hour training is the beginning of a teacher’s education, not its end. Most teachers find that the first year of teaching raises more questions than the training answered. Plan for:

- **Mentoring**: regular contact with an experienced teacher who observes you and whom you can consult.
- **Assisting**: assisting an experienced teacher’s classes is one of the best ways to learn observation and adjustment.
- **Continuing education**: workshops and further trainings in areas you need (anatomy, prenatal yoga, yoga for older adults, prāṇāyāma, meditation, philosophy). Advanced trainings (e.g. 300- or 500-hour programmes) deepen knowledge; yoga therapy is a separate, longer clinical training.
- **Study**: the reading list in Appendix M, and returning to this manual.
- **Your own practice** — the foundation of all the rest.

## Credentials and recognition

Graduates of this course receive the **Santhi Yoga School 200-hour certificate**. The school is not a Yoga Alliance Registered Yoga School. Many studios and retreats accept certificates from established schools; some employers, particularly outside India, ask specifically for registration with a particular body. **Check the requirements of the places where you intend to teach**, and plan any further training accordingly. Represent your qualifications accurately at all times (*satya*).

## Practical foundations

Table: Practical foundations of a teaching practice {#tab:practical}

| Area | What to do |
|---|---|
| First aid | hold a current first-aid certificate and renew it as required |
| Insurance | obtain professional liability insurance where available and where you teach; studios may require it |
| Legal and tax | find out the rules for teaching, business registration, tax and visas in each country where you teach; never teach on a visa that does not permit work |
| Health records | keep student health questionnaires securely and confidentially; know how long records should be kept locally |
| Incident records | record any injury, illness or complaint, with date, facts and actions taken |
| Contracts | written agreements with studios (fees, cancellation, cover, insurance, intellectual property) |
| Data and privacy | handle personal data carefully; follow local data-protection law |

## Marketing with integrity

Marketing is simply telling people honestly what you offer, so that the right students can find you.

- **Describe what you teach**, your training and your lineage accurately.
- **Make no health promises** or unsupported claims (“cures back pain”, “detoxes the body”) — they breach *satya* and may breach consumer or advertising law.
- **Use images respectfully**: consent from anyone photographed; representative, diverse images; no sacred symbols as decoration.
- **Build relationships**, not just followers: word of mouth from students who feel well taught remains the strongest marketing.
- **Price fairly**: know your costs; consider concessions; avoid pressure-selling.

## A sustainable working life

Teaching yoga full-time is physically, vocally and emotionally demanding, and often financially precarious. Sustainable teachers tend to:

- **limit the number of classes** per week to what they can teach well — often fewer than new teachers expect;
- **not practise every class they teach** — demonstrate selectively and keep separate time for their own practice;
- **diversify income**: group classes, privates, workshops, retreats, online, corporate and community teaching;
- **protect the voice and body** (@ch:teaching-skills);
- **take real rest**: days off and holidays;
- **stay connected** with a community of teachers;
- **seek support** early when tired, disillusioned or overwhelmed.

::: keyconcept Karma yoga and a living
The Gītā’s teaching of action without attachment to results (@ch:gita) is not a reason to be exploited or to undervalue your work. It is a way of doing your work — fully, well, and without letting praise, numbers or income define your worth — while still earning a fair living from it.
:::

::: exercise A one-year plan
Write a one-year plan with four sections: **practice** (your daily practice commitment), **learning** (two areas of study, one mentor), **teaching** (where, how often, whom), and **sustainability** (rest, income, support). Review it with your mentor at the end of the course.
:::

::: takeaways
- A 200-hour training begins a teacher’s education; plan mentoring, assisting, continuing education and practice.
- Know what your certificate is and is not; check the requirements where you intend to teach; represent qualifications truthfully.
- Establish practical foundations: first aid, insurance, legal and tax compliance, records, contracts, data protection.
- Market honestly, without health promises, with respectful images and fair pricing.
- Sustainability depends on limiting teaching load, diversifying income, protecting body and voice, rest and community.
:::
