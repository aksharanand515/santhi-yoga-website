**Santhi School of Yoga & Vedanta Studies** — Santhi Yoga School, or simply *Santhi* (Malayalam <span lang="ml">ശാന്തി</span>, Sanskrit *śānti*, “peace”) — is a traditional Hatha Yoga school in Chirattapalam, in the old town of Fort Kochi, Kerala. Classes are held every day of the year in the school’s garden pavilion, and the 200-hour teacher training runs as a residential course of twenty-eight days.

The school is led by **Master Aksharanand A S**, known to his students as Achu, who trained in the Sivananda tradition and holds an Advanced Teacher Training Certificate. Over more than fifteen years of teaching in Fort Kochi he has taught more than five thousand group classes and led more than seventy international teacher-training batches, and he teaches Hatha Yoga, prāṇāyāma, meditation and Vedānta philosophy on the course.

### What we teach

The daily class at Santhi follows the traditional order of the Sivananda sequence: an opening relaxation, prāṇāyāma, Sūrya Namaskār, the classical postures held with attention and breath, and a long final relaxation. The teacher training grows out of that class. Over twenty-eight days you study:

- **Hatha Yoga** — the traditional practice at the heart of the school, including the twelve basic postures;
- **Vinyasa** — linking movement and breath into flowing sequences;
- **Āsana** — postures, alignment, anatomy and sequencing in detail;
- **Prāṇāyāma and meditation** — practised every day;
- **Yoga philosophy** — the Upaniṣads, the Bhagavad Gītā, the Yoga Sūtras and Vedānta;
- **Teaching methodology** — how to guide, cue, observe and hold space for students.

### Where the school stands

Santhi teaches in the lineage of Swami Sivananda of Rishikesh and his disciple Swami Vishnudevananda, who was born in Kerala in 1927 and founded the Sivananda Yoga Vedanta Centres. We are grateful to that lineage and faithful to its methods, but Santhi is an **independent school** and is not an official Sivananda Yoga Vedanta Centre. Santhi is also **not a Yoga Alliance Registered Yoga School**; graduates receive the school’s own 200-hour certificate. We would rather say both of these things plainly than let a student discover them later.
