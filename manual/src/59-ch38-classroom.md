::: objectives
By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

- set up and manage a practice space, before, during and after class;
- establish clear, welcoming class agreements;
- respond calmly to common difficult situations: lateness, phones, talking, competitiveness, disruptive or distressed students, unwanted attention and medical events;
- reflect on difficult situations and seek support.
:::

## Before class

- **Arrive early.** Prepare the room: clean floor, ventilation, lighting, temperature, props laid out, water available, first-aid kit accessible.
- **Arrange the mats** so that you can see everyone and everyone can see you (@fig:classroom), with space to walk between them.
- **Greet students** at the door; learn names; ask newcomers about health and experience.
- **Set the tone**: calm, unhurried, welcoming.

## Class agreements

Students relax when they know what to expect. State the basics briefly — or post them — and follow them yourself:

- arrive a few minutes early; if late, enter quietly (many schools do not admit students after the opening relaxation, for safety and for others’ concentration);
- phones silent and away;
- tell the teacher about injuries, conditions or pregnancy;
- rest whenever you need to; follow your own body;
- stay for the final relaxation — or, if you must leave early, tell the teacher and leave before it begins.

## Common situations

Table: Responding to common difficult situations {#tab:difficult}

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| **Late arrival** | welcome quietly; guide to a space; after class, explain the reason for arriving on time (safety, warm-up, others’ focus). If late arrivals would miss the warm-up before strong postures, offer a gentler alternative |
| **Phone rings** | a neutral reminder to the whole room at the start of the next class, not public embarrassment |
| **Talking during class** | a general, friendly reminder (“let’s keep a quiet room for everyone”); speak privately afterwards if it continues |
| **Competitive or over-striving student** | offer options to the whole class; praise steadiness and breath rather than depth; speak privately about safety if needed |
| **Student ignores safety instructions** | calm, private, clear explanation of the risk; if they persist in something dangerous, ask them to stop — safety of the student and others comes first |
| **Student is distressed or tearful** | allow space; offer quiet reassurance; invite rest, eyes open, grounding; check in privately afterwards; refer if appropriate |
| **Student gives unsolicited advice to others** | thank them, and gently reclaim the teaching role (“let’s let everyone follow their own practice today”) |
| **Unwanted personal attention towards the teacher or other students** | clear, calm boundary-setting; involve the school leadership; never ignore harassment |
| **A student feels faint or unwell** | help them to lie down (or sit with head low), legs raised if faint; stay with them; seek medical help if symptoms do not settle or are serious (@ch:safety) |
| **Emergency** | follow the emergency plan: call for help, give first aid within your training, assign another adult to manage the rest of the class |
| **You make a mistake** | acknowledge it simply, correct it, continue; learning from mistakes models what yoga teaches |

::: tip Private, not public
Almost every correction of behaviour is better given privately, after class, than publicly during it. Public correction embarrasses the student and unsettles the room; private conversation preserves dignity and relationship.
:::

## Hot, crowded and noisy rooms

Fort Kochi is not a silent monastery. Traffic, temple loudspeakers, crows, rain on the roof and the heat of the afternoon are part of practice here. Teach students to include sounds in their awareness rather than resent them (@ch:limbs-outer). Adjust the practice for heat, and reduce numbers rather than crowd mats.

## Looking after yourself

Difficult situations are stressful. Afterwards:

- **write a brief note** of what happened and what you did (especially for any incident involving injury, illness or complaint);
- **talk to a colleague or mentor**;
- **reflect** without self-blame: what would you do the same, what differently?

::: takeaways
- Prepare the room and yourself before class; greet students and learn names.
- Clear, welcoming agreements reduce problems; model them yourself.
- Respond to difficulties calmly, generally first and privately after; safety always comes first.
- Manage fainting, distress and emergencies with a clear plan.
- Record incidents, talk to a mentor and reflect afterwards.
:::
