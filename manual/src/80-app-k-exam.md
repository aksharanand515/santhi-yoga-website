The final examination has two parts: a **written paper** (2 hours 30 minutes, closed book) and a **practical teaching examination** (@ch:practicum). This appendix gives the structure of both and a complete sample written paper for practice. The paper set on the course follows the same pattern with different questions.

## The written paper {#k-written}

Table: Structure of the written paper {#tab:k-structure}

| Part | Format | Marks | Suggested time |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 30 multiple-choice questions, one mark each | 30 | 30 min |
| B | 12 short-answer questions (3–5 sentences), three marks each | 36 | 60 min |
| C | 2 extended answers from a choice of 6, 17 marks each | 34 | 60 min |
| | **Total** | **100** | 2 h 30 min |

The pass mark is 70, with at least 50 per cent in each part. A candidate who does not pass may re-sit once.

## Sample paper — Part A: multiple choice {#k-part-a}

Choose the single best answer.

**Philosophy and tradition**

1. Yoga Sūtra 1.2 defines yoga as: (a) balance of sun and moon (b) the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind (c) union with God (d) skill in action
2. The five kleśas, in the order given by Patañjali, begin with: (a) asmitā (b) avidyā (c) dveṣa (d) rāga
3. Which is **not** one of the yamas? (a) ahiṃsā (b) asteya (c) aparigraha (d) santoṣa
4. The Bhagavad Gītā is part of the: (a) Mahābhārata (b) Ṛg Veda (c) Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā (d) Rāmāyaṇa
5. Most scholars date the Yoga Sūtra to approximately: (a) the 15th century CE (b) 325–425 CE (c) 1500 BCE (d) 800 BCE
6. The *Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā* was composed by: (a) Śaṅkara (b) Patañjali (c) Svātmārāma (d) Vyāsa
7. The five points of yoga taught by Swami Vishnudevananda are proper exercise, proper breathing, proper relaxation, proper diet and: (a) proper chanting (b) proper study (c) proper sleep (d) positive thinking and meditation
8. The kośa associated with the breath and vital energy is the: (a) prāṇamaya (b) vijñānamaya (c) manomaya (d) annamaya
9. The guṇa of clarity and harmony is: (a) prakṛti (b) rajas (c) sattva (d) tamas
10. Karma yoga is best described as: (a) action performed without attachment to its results (b) yoga of knowledge (c) yoga of devotion (d) meditation on the chakras

**Anatomy, physiology and safety**

11. Flexion and extension occur in the: (a) transverse plane (b) coronal plane (c) sagittal plane (d) frontal plane
12. The principal muscle of quiet inhalation is the: (a) pectoralis major (b) rectus abdominis (c) diaphragm (d) sternocleidomastoid
13. The hamstrings originate on the: (a) sacrum (b) ischial tuberosity (c) greater trochanter (d) ASIS
14. In paścimottānāsana, the hamstrings are mainly: (a) contracting concentrically (b) unchanged (c) shortening (d) lengthening
15. Iliopsoas is primarily a: (a) hip flexor (b) shoulder adductor (c) hip extensor (d) knee extensor
16. The rotator cuff includes all of these **except**: (a) supraspinatus (b) infraspinatus (c) deltoid (d) subscapularis
17. The normal curve of the lumbar spine is a: (a) lordosis (b) flat back (c) scoliosis (d) kyphosis
18. Respiratory sinus arrhythmia describes: (a) an irregular breathing pattern (b) heart rate rising with inhalation and falling with exhalation (c) a heart disease (d) hyperventilation
19. Prolonged exhalation is generally associated with: (a) sympathetic dominance (b) hypocapnia (c) increased parasympathetic (vagal) influence (d) raised blood pressure
20. A student feels tingling in the hands and light-headedness during fast breathing. The most likely cause is: (a) a heart attack (b) low oxygen (c) low carbon dioxide from over-breathing (d) high blood pressure
21. Which is a contraindication for full inversions without medical advice? (a) tight hamstrings (b) flat feet (c) being a beginner at meditation (d) uncontrolled high blood pressure or glaucoma
22. In a mixed class, a student with osteoporosis should avoid: (a) breathing exercises (b) all practice (c) standing postures (d) loaded spinal flexion such as deep rounded forward bends
23. Radiating pain, numbness or weakness down a leg during practice means: (a) switch sides (b) stop and refer to a health professional (c) hold longer (d) stretch further

**Teaching methodology**

24. The best time to cue an upward movement is usually on the: (a) exhalation (b) retention (c) inhalation (d) it makes no difference
25. A counterpose for shoulderstand in the Sivananda sequence is: (a) fish (b) plough (c) headstand (d) cobra
26. When adjusting a student, the teacher must first: (a) check the time (b) obtain consent (c) demonstrate (d) apply firm pressure
27. Offering tiered options in a mixed-level class aims to: (a) speed the class (b) separate students by ability (c) avoid demonstration (d) let each student find an appropriate version safely
28. Trauma-sensitive teaching includes: (a) closing all doors and dimming lights without explanation (b) surprising students with adjustments (c) offering choice and invitational language (d) requiring eyes closed
29. Which wording is appropriate for a class description? (a) “A calm class of postures, breathing and relaxation” (b) “Detoxifies the liver” (c) “Cures anxiety” (d) “Guaranteed to fix back pain”
30. A student asks for advice about stopping their medication. You should: (a) ignore the question (b) refer them to their doctor (c) tell them to stop (d) advise them based on yoga philosophy

## Sample paper — Part B: short answers {#k-part-b}

Answer each question in three to five sentences.

1. Distinguish between *abhyāsa* and *vairāgya* and explain how they work together (YS 1.12–16).
2. Name the eight limbs of Patañjali’s yoga and explain why the yamas and niyamas come first.
3. Explain, in plain language, the teaching of *niṣkāma karma* in the Bhagavad Gītā and give an example from a yoga teacher’s life.
4. What is the difference between a historical claim, a traditional teaching and a scientific finding? Give one example of each from this course.
5. Describe the five kośas and suggest how a single yoga class might address each.
6. Describe the actions of the diaphragm during inhalation and exhalation, and one practical implication for teaching breath.
7. Explain why tight hamstrings can lead to lumbar flexion in seated forward bends, and give two modifications.
8. List four precautions for teaching sarvāṅgāsana, and describe one safe alternative.
9. What are the signs that a student should stop a prāṇāyāma practice, and what should they do?
10. Describe three ways of making a class accessible to a student who cannot get down to the floor.
11. Explain the stages of motor learning and how they should shape the way you teach a new posture.
12. What is informed consent for touch, and how would you obtain it in a group class?

## Sample paper — Part C: extended answers {#k-part-c}

Choose **two** questions. Plan your answer; aim for a clear structure of about one and a half to two pages each. Credit is given for accuracy, use of sources, balance between tradition and evidence, and practical application to teaching.

1. “Yoga is the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind.” Explain this definition with reference to the vṛttis, the kleśas and the practices Patañjali recommends. How is it relevant to a modern āsana class?
2. Compare the paths of karma, bhakti, jñāna and rāja yoga. How does Swami Sivananda’s “yoga of synthesis” bring them together, and how might a teacher reflect it in a 90-minute class?
3. Choose one of the twelve basic postures. Describe its technique, the joint actions and principal muscles involved, common mistakes, contraindications, modifications and a suitable counterpose. Explain what the evidence does and does not tell us about its effects.
4. Discuss the evidence for yoga’s effects on the autonomic nervous system and stress. Distinguish what is well supported, what is plausible, and what is overclaimed, and explain how you would speak about it to students.
5. Plan a 75-minute class for a mixed group including a beginner, a student in the second trimester of pregnancy, and a regular practitioner with a history of low-back pain. Justify your sequencing, options and safety measures.
6. What makes a teacher trustworthy? Discuss the ethics of the teacher–student relationship, including power, boundaries, touch and scope of practice, with reference to the yamas and niyamas and to documented problems in modern yoga communities.

## The practical teaching examination {#k-practical}

**Task.** Teach a 45- to 60-minute class (as assigned) to a group of fellow trainees and/or members of the public, following the structure of the Sivananda class or a plan of your own approved in advance.

**Submit** your written lesson plan (@ch:class-planning) to the assessors 24 hours before the examination.

**Assessment** uses the practicum assessment form in Appendix I against the criteria in @tab:exam-criteria. Assessors look for safe, clear, well-sequenced teaching adapted to the people present — not for a perfect performance.

**Reflection.** Immediately after the class, you will have ten minutes to discuss with the assessors what went well, what you would change, and why.

::: tip Preparing for the written paper
Revise from the Key Takeaways and Knowledge Checks at the end of each chapter; test yourself with the glossaries (Appendices A and B); and practise explaining one idea from each chapter aloud to a classmate in two minutes. If you can explain it simply, you understand it.
:::
