Six complete class plans built on the principles of @ch:sequencing and @ch:class-planning. Each is a model to learn from and adapt, not a fixed routine. Timings are approximate; protect the final relaxation whatever else you shorten. Before every class, ask about injuries, health conditions and pregnancy, and offer the options shown.

## The classical class (90 minutes) {#g-classical}

The open class as taught at the school (@ch:twelve-asanas). Its fixed order — relaxation, breath, sun salutation, leg raises, the twelve postures from inversions to standing poses, and deep relaxation — is itself a sequencing lesson: each posture prepares for or counterbalances its neighbours.

!sequence(@classical)

Table: The classical open class, 90 minutes {#tab:g-classical}

| Time | Practice | Notes and options |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Opening chant; relaxation in śavāsana | abdominal breathing; arrive and settle |
| 5–20 | Kapālabhāti (3 rounds); anuloma viloma (5–10 rounds) | beginners omit retention; seated on a cushion or chair |
| 20–30 | Sūrya namaskār, 6–12 rounds | slow first rounds; knee-down option |
| 30–35 | Single and double leg raises; relaxation | bend the knees; hands under the pelvis if the low back lifts |
| 35–43 | Śīrṣāsana or its preparation; child’s pose | dolphin for those not ready; never a first-class pose |
| 43–53 | Sarvāṅgāsana, halāsana, matsyāsana | blanket under the shoulders; plough feet on a chair; supported fish |
| 53–58 | Paścimottānāsana; inclined plane | strap; blanket under the pelvis |
| 58–66 | Bhujaṅgāsana, śalabhāsana, dhanurāsana; child’s pose | half locust, half bow; rest between |
| 66–70 | Ardha matsyendrāsana | easy seated twist |
| 70–73 | Kākāsana or mayūrāsana | squat (mālāsana) as preparation |
| 73–76 | Pāda hastāsana | knees bent; hands on shins |
| 76–79 | Trikoṇāsana | block; hand on shin |
| 79–90 | Final relaxation: tense-and-release, then guided relaxation and silence; closing chant | a blanket for warmth; roll to the side to come up |

## A 60-minute general class {#g-sixty}

A compressed version of the classical order for studios and lunchtime classes. Fewer rounds, shorter holds, and alternatives to the full inversions — but the same arc (@fig:classarc).

!sequence(@sixty)

Table: A 60-minute general class {#tab:g-sixty}

| Time | Practice | Key cues | Options |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Relaxation | “Let the floor take your weight.” | knees bent on a bolster |
| 5–15 | Kapālabhāti 2 rounds; anuloma viloma 5 rounds | “Smooth and silent.” | no retention |
| 15–22 | Sūrya namaskār, 4–6 rounds | “Move with the breath.” | knee-down lunge |
| 22–25 | Leg raises | “Lower slowly.” | single leg only |
| 25–29 | Dolphin / headstand preparation | “Press the forearms down.” | child’s pose |
| 29–35 | Shoulderstand, plough | “Keep the head still.” | legs up the wall; feet on chair |
| 35–37 | Fish | “Weight on the elbows.” | supported fish |
| 37–40 | Seated forward bend | “Fold from the hips.” | strap, blanket |
| 40–45 | Cobra, half locust, bow | “Lift with the back.” | sphinx; half bow |
| 45–47 | Half spinal twist | “Grow tall, then turn.” | easy twist |
| 47–50 | Standing forward bend, triangle | “Reach out, then down.” | block |
| 50–60 | Final relaxation | “Nothing to do.” | eyes open if preferred |

## A 90-minute general class with a theme {#g-ninety}

**Theme:** *Sthira sukham āsanam* — steadiness and ease (@ch:limbs-outer). **Intention:** to notice where effort is needed and where it can be released. Revisit the theme in a sentence or two at the opening, once in the middle, and at the close; do not lecture.

Table: A 90-minute themed class {#tab:g-ninety}

| Time | Section | Practice | Teaching focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–8 | Arrival | relaxation; introduce the theme in two sentences | voice, pacing, silence |
| 8–23 | Breath | full yogic breath; kapālabhāti 3 rounds; anuloma viloma 8 rounds | steady breath as the model of *sthira* and *sukha* |
| 23–28 | Warm-up | cat–cow, low lunge, gentle twists | mobilise spine and hips |
| 28–38 | Sūrya namaskār | 3 slow rounds taught in detail, then 6 flowing | even breath throughout |
| 38–42 | Leg raises | single, then double | core steadiness; soft face |
| 42–52 | Inversions | headstand tiers; child’s pose | steadiness first, then ease |
| 52–62 | Shoulderstand group | shoulderstand, plough, fish | neck safety; ease in the breath |
| 62–68 | Forward bends | paścimottānāsana; janu śīrṣāsana | ease: waiting rather than pulling |
| 68–76 | Backbends | cobra, locust, bow | steadiness: strength from the back |
| 76–79 | Twist | ardha matsyendrāsana | length, then rotation |
| 79–82 | Balance | crow preparation or vṛkṣāsana | steady gaze |
| 82–85 | Standing | pāda hastāsana, trikoṇāsana | even weight |
| 85–90 | Relaxation and close | guided relaxation; one sentence on the theme; silence | ending on time |

## A beginners’ class (60–75 minutes) {#g-beginner}

For a first course of four to six weeks. Teach less and explain more; introduce one new element each week; give every posture a simpler option and time to rest (@ch:levels).

!sequence(@beginner)

Table: A beginners’ class, week 2 of a 6-week course {#tab:g-beginner}

| Time | Practice | Why / teaching notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 | Relaxation; abdominal breathing with a hand on the belly | teaches diaphragmatic breathing by feel |
| 10–18 | Full yogic breath; nāḍī śodhana without retention, 5 rounds | introduce Viṣṇu mudrā; allow the free hand to rest |
| 18–22 | Cat–cow; low lunge | warms the spine and hips before sūrya namaskār |
| 22–32 | Sūrya namaskār taught in three parts, then 3 slow rounds | demonstrate once; then teach from observation |
| 32–35 | Single leg raises | knees soft; lower back relaxed |
| 35–40 | Dolphin; child’s pose | builds shoulder strength for later headstand |
| 40–46 | Viparīta karaṇī or legs up the wall; bridge; supported fish | introduces inversion and its counterpose safely |
| 46–50 | Forward bend with strap | “fold from the hips” |
| 50–56 | Sphinx; half locust; child’s pose | back strength without load on the arms |
| 56–59 | Easy twist | sitting on a cushion |
| 59–63 | Standing forward bend with bent knees; triangle with block | stable, accessible standing poses |
| 63–75 | Final relaxation with tense-and-release | the practice students most often remember |

## An intermediate class (90 minutes) {#g-intermediate}

For students who practise regularly, hold the basic postures steadily, and have learned headstand with a teacher. Longer holds, retention in prāṇāyāma, and one or two variations per posture group.

!sequence(@intermediate)

Table: An intermediate class {#tab:g-intermediate}

| Time | Practice | Progression |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Relaxation | shorter; students settle quickly |
| 5–25 | Kapālabhāti 3 × 40–60; anuloma viloma with retention 1:4:2, 10 rounds | stop if strain appears; count adapted to capacity |
| 25–35 | Sūrya namaskār, 12 rounds | steady pace, continuous breath |
| 35–40 | Double leg raises | lower slowly on the exhalation |
| 40–48 | Śīrṣāsana 3–5 min, with leg variations for those ready | child’s pose after |
| 48–60 | Sarvāṅgāsana 3–5 min; halāsana; bridge from shoulderstand for those ready; matsyāsana | counterpose held half the time of shoulderstand |
| 60–66 | Paścimottānāsana; nāvāsana | forward bend, then abdominal strength |
| 66–74 | Bhujaṅgāsana, full śalabhāsana, dhanurāsana, uṣṭrāsana | child’s pose between |
| 74–77 | Ardha matsyendrāsana | full arm bind if available |
| 77–80 | Mayūrāsana or kākāsana | wrist preparation first |
| 80–84 | Pāda hastāsana; trikoṇāsana; vṛkṣāsana | balance with eyes closed for a few breaths |
| 84–90 | Final relaxation | silence for the last minutes |

## A mixed-level class (75 minutes) {#g-mixed}

Plan every posture in **tiers** (@ch:levels): demonstrate or describe the first tier, then offer the next. Name tiers neutrally (“option one, option two”), never “easy” and “advanced”. Keep inversions optional and give a clear alternative to everyone who does not do them.

!sequence(@mixed)

Table: A mixed-level class — tiered options {#tab:g-mixed}

| Posture group | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prāṇāyāma | abdominal breath, nāḍī śodhana | anuloma viloma 1:2 | with retention |
| Sūrya namaskār | knee-down lunge and plank | full, slow | full, 12 rounds |
| Headstand | dolphin | knees tucked | full pose |
| Shoulderstand | legs up the wall | half shoulderstand | full pose |
| Plough | omitted or feet on chair | feet on floor | — |
| Fish | supported on bolster | classic, weight on elbows | lotus legs |
| Forward bend | strap, knees bent | hands to shins or feet | full fold |
| Backbends | sphinx, half locust | cobra, full locust | bow |
| Twist | easy seated twist | half spinal twist | full bind |
| Arm balance | squat | crow, one foot lifted | crow |
| Standing | forward bend, knees bent; triangle with block | classic forms | hold longer |

## A restorative interlude {#g-restorative}

For tired, stressed, unwell or recovering students — or as the second half of a workshop. Hold each pose 3–8 minutes with ample props; keep the room warm; talk little.

!sequence(@restorative)

::: tip Adapting any plan
Before class, ask: *Who is coming? What do they need today?* After class, write three lines in your practicum log (Appendix I): what worked, what did not, what you will change.
:::
