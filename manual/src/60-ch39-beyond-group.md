::: objectives
By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

- conduct an initial consultation and plan a private class or series;
- design and deliver a workshop with clear learning outcomes;
- teach live online classes safely and effectively;
- recognise the particular ethical and safety considerations of each format.
:::

## Private classes

A private class is the most individual form of teaching — close to the traditional one-to-one relationship between teacher and student.

### The first session

1. **Consultation (10–15 minutes).** Ask about goals, experience, health, injuries, medication relevant to exercise, pregnancy, lifestyle and what the student enjoys. Use a written health questionnaire (Appendix I).
2. **Observation.** Watch the student in a few simple movements — standing, forward bend, a lunge, sitting, breathing — without correcting.
3. **Practice.** Teach a short, safe, appropriate practice.
4. **Home practice.** Give a simple routine (10–20 minutes) they can remember, with a written or drawn sheet.
5. **Agree next steps.** Frequency, goals, how progress will be recognised.

### Planning a series

Set realistic goals with the student — for example, a steady home practice, comfort sitting for meditation, preparation for headstand. Build progressively, review regularly and keep notes. Private students often have specific health goals; remember your scope (@ch:teacher-role) and liaise with their healthcare professional where appropriate.

::: safety Privates and boundaries
Private sessions happen in private spaces — the student’s home, a hotel room, a small studio. Professional boundaries matter even more (@ch:ethics): clear appointments and payment, a professional setting where possible, touch only with explicit consent, and no social or romantic entanglement. Tell someone where you are when teaching in unfamiliar private settings.
:::

## Workshops

A workshop (typically 2–3 hours, or a day) explores one topic in depth: inversions, backbends, prāṇāyāma, the Bhagavad Gītā, yoga for runners.

**Designing a workshop:**

- **Title and outcomes.** Write two to four outcomes: “By the end, participants will be able to…”.
- **Structure.** Arrival and practice; teaching segments alternating with practice; partner or small-group work; a closing practice and questions.
- **Variety.** Mix explanation, demonstration, practice, discussion and reflection; change activity every 15–20 minutes.
- **Handout.** A one-page summary students can take home.
- **Prerequisites and safety.** State the level and any contraindications in advance (e.g. an inversion workshop).

## Online teaching

Live online classes extend teaching beyond the room, but change it.

Table: Adapting to online teaching {#tab:online}

| Consideration | Good practice |
|---|---|
| Screening | health questionnaire before the first class; emergency contact and address for each student; consent form |
| Visibility | ask students to place the camera to see the whole body; demonstrate side-on to the camera for sagittal postures |
| Cueing | more verbal precision; say “right” and “left” clearly; describe what you are showing |
| Observation | watch the gallery view; name individuals for feedback only with consent; unmute for questions |
| Safety | teach less demanding postures than in person; no inversions for new students; props from household items |
| Technology | stable connection, good microphone, lighting in front of you; a backup plan |
| Privacy | no recording without explicit consent; secure meeting links; respect students’ homes |
| Emergencies | know each student’s location; if a student is unwell, contact their emergency contact or local services |

::: tip Household props
Books for blocks, a belt or scarf for a strap, a folded blanket or firm cushion, a sturdy chair, a wall. Name the substitutes in advance so students can prepare.
:::

::: takeaways
- Private classes begin with consultation and observation, and progress through a planned series with home practice.
- Workshops have clear outcomes, varied activities, handouts and stated prerequisites.
- Online teaching needs screening, emergency information, precise verbal cues, careful observation, safer content and respect for privacy.
- Each format carries specific boundary and safety considerations.
:::
