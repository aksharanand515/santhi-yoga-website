Two model sessions, with a guided script, that trainees teach during the practicum. The techniques, contraindications and evidence are in @ch:pranayama and @ch:meditation-practice. Read the scripts slowly, pausing where marked (…); the pauses are part of the teaching.

## A 30-minute prāṇāyāma session {#h-pranayama}

**Suitable for:** generally healthy beginners and mixed groups. **Setting:** seated on a cushion, bench or chair, spine upright and comfortable; empty stomach (two to three hours after a meal). **Screening:** ask about pregnancy, high blood pressure, heart disease, recent surgery, glaucoma, epilepsy and panic or anxiety disorders; offer the gentle options below.

Table: A 30-minute prāṇāyāma session {#tab:h-pranayama}

| Time | Practice | Instructions | Options and safety |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | Settling | sit comfortably; observe the natural breath | chair, wall support |
| 4–9 | Abdominal and full yogic breath | 10 slow breaths of each; hands on belly, then ribs | no forcing; shoulders relaxed |
| 9–15 | Kapālabhāti | 3 rounds of 20–40 exhalations, each followed by 3–4 normal breaths | omit in pregnancy, hypertension, recent abdominal surgery, dizziness; replace with slow abdominal breathing |
| 15–25 | Anuloma viloma / nāḍī śodhana | beginners: 4:8 (in:out), no retention; experienced: 1:4:2 | never strain in retention; omit retention in pregnancy, hypertension, heart disease, anxiety |
| 25–28 | Bhrāmarī | 5–7 rounds of humming exhalation | quiet version if self-conscious |
| 28–30 | Silence | sit and observe; roll the shoulders; open the eyes slowly | — |

::: plain Script: introducing nāḍī śodhana
Bring the right hand up and fold down the first two fingers, so that the thumb, ring finger and little finger are free … This is Viṣṇu mudrā. Let the left hand rest on the thigh … Close the right nostril with the thumb, and breathe out slowly through the left … Breathe in through the left for a count of four … Close the left nostril with the ring finger; release the thumb and breathe out through the right for a count of eight … Breathe in through the right … close … and breathe out through the left … That is one round. If the count feels long, shorten it: the breath should stay smooth and silent … Let the arm rest whenever it tires.
:::

::: safety Stop signals
Stop any prāṇāyāma and breathe normally if you feel dizzy, light-headed, breathless, tingling in the hands or face, anxious, or unwell. These are usually signs of over-breathing or strain (@ch:pranayama).
:::

## A 25-minute guided meditation session {#h-meditation}

**Suitable for:** beginners and mixed groups. **Object:** the breath, with an optional mantra. **Structure:** posture → body scan → breath awareness → one-pointed attention → open awareness → close. Invite students to keep their eyes open or lowered if they prefer, and remind them that they can move or stop at any time (@ch:inclusive).

Table: A 25-minute meditation session {#tab:h-meditation}

| Time | Stage | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Posture | stable, upright, comfortable; hands resting; eyes closed or lowered |
| 3–7 | Body scan | from the feet to the head, noticing and softening |
| 7–12 | Breath awareness | the breath at the nostrils or abdomen; counting 1–10 if helpful |
| 12–20 | One-pointed attention | resting on the breath (or mantra: *so* on inhalation, *ham* on exhalation, or Oṃ); returning each time the mind wanders |
| 20–23 | Open awareness | releasing the object; resting as the witness of sounds, sensations and thoughts |
| 23–25 | Closing | deepen the breath; move gently; open the eyes; a short sharing if appropriate |

::: plain Script: returning to the breath
Rest your attention on the breath where you feel it most clearly … at the nostrils, or the rise and fall of the belly … There is no need to change it … Sooner or later the mind will wander — into planning, remembering, imagining. This is not a mistake; it is what minds do … The moment you notice that you have wandered is a moment of awareness. Gently, without judging, come back to the next breath … and again … and again. Each return is the practice.
:::

::: tip After the session
Allow a minute of silence before speaking. If you invite sharing, keep it optional and brief. Some students may feel emotional or unsettled; stay available after class and know when to suggest other support (@ch:meditation-practice, @ch:adjustments on trauma-aware teaching).
:::

## A 20-minute yoga nidrā outline {#h-nidra}

A simplified outline in the style popularised by the Bihar School (@satyananda1976). Students lie in śavāsana with a blanket; guide in a steady voice with long pauses.

1. **Settling** (2 min): body position, stillness, sounds from far to near.
2. **Saṅkalpa** (1 min): a short, positive resolve in the student’s own words, repeated three times mentally.
3. **Rotation of consciousness** (6 min): naming body parts in sequence, right side then left, back, front, whole body.
4. **Breath awareness** (4 min): natural breath; counting backwards from 27 to 1.
5. **Sensations and images** (3 min): heaviness and lightness, warmth and coolness; a few simple, peaceful images (optional — some students prefer to omit visualisation).
6. **Saṅkalpa** (1 min): repeat the resolve.
7. **Return** (3 min): awareness of the breath, the body, the room; move slowly; roll to the side; sit up.
