02What you will study

## A traditional *foundation*

The 28 days bring together daily practice, study, learning how to guide others, and life in Fort Kochi.

1. ### Hatha Yoga

   The traditional practice at the heart of the school: a two-hour class that opens with relaxation and breathing, moves through sun salutations and held postures with rest between them, and ends in deep relaxation.
2. ### Vinyasa

   Linking movement and breath into flowing sequences.
3. ### Asana

   Postures, alignment and sequencing in detail, with corrections given by hand rather than called across the room.
4. ### Pranayama

   Breathing techniques, practised every morning at dawn, with attention and without strain.
5. ### Meditation

   Stillness and reflection as part of every day.
6. ### Yoga philosophy

   The traditional teachings behind the practice, including the Vedanta philosophy the school is named for, taught in plain words.
7. ### Teaching methodology

   How to guide, cue, observe and hold space for students, then practice teaching your fellow trainees, with feedback, from the third week.
8. ### Kerala living

   Accommodation in Fort Kochi, sattvic meals and cultural excursions.

03The month

## How the 28 days *unfold*

Two hundred hours in 28 days is a full-time month, six days a week, with one day free each week to rest and go to the sea. This is how our own course runs.

1. Days 1–7

   ### Week one

   Everything is new. You learn the daily sequence, the breathing practices and the shape of the day, while your body adjusts to two practices and the Kerala heat. Almost everyone wonders whether they are fit enough; it passes.
2. Days 8–14

   ### Week two

   The body adapts and the timetable stops feeling like an imposition. This is often when the philosophy classes start to land.
3. Days 15–21

   ### Week three

   You begin teaching your fellow trainees in small groups: one of you teaches, the others practise and then say what worked. It is the hardest week, and the most useful.
4. Days 22–28

   ### Week four

   Final taught classes and assessment: a class you teach and a written paper. Then the certificate, and the strange, full feeling of a month that went quickly.

07Yoga Alliance

## Are we registered with *Yoga Alliance?*

**No, and that is a conscious choice.**

We believe yoga education should be rooted in India’s traditional yoga system, not defined solely by an international accreditation framework.

While many Yoga Alliance-registered schools offer their 200-hour training over approximately 21 days, we offer a 28-day immersive residential programme to give students more time for practice, study and a deeper understanding of yoga beyond asana.

Our approach is grounded in traditional knowledge, years of teaching experience and a genuine commitment to making quality yoga education accessible at a reasonable price.

We respect different approaches to yoga education, but our priority is clear: depth over speed, authentic practice over certificates, and meaningful learning over ticking boxes.

If you seek a Yoga Alliance credential, our programme may not be the right fit. If you seek an immersive experience rooted in Indian yoga tradition, we welcome you to join us.

09The place

## Why do your teacher training *in Kerala*

Kerala suits a teacher training with a gentler daily rhythm: warm weather all year, Ayurveda part of everyday life, and far fewer other trainees around than in Rishikesh. Teaching quality overlaps almost completely between the two; the atmosphere does not. Rishikesh offers more structure, devotional immersion and the widest choice of schools. Kerala offers room to breathe.

Fort Kochi adds an old harbour town to live in for a month: Chinese fishing nets, colonial lanes, the Kathakali theatres and the sea, all within reach on your free day. The school is a working school in the same seaside quarter where it has taught for more than fifteen years, not a resort that hosts a course.

### Fort Kochi or Varkala?

Many of Kerala’s teacher trainings run in Varkala. Ours is in Fort Kochi, and the two feel very different. Varkala is a cliff-top beach town, with many courses based in guesthouses and resorts along the cliff and the sea in view all day. Fort Kochi is an old port town where people live and work all year: you walk to class past the fishing nets, old Portuguese and Dutch houses and spice warehouses, and the evenings bring Kathakali and, around New Year, the carnival. Both are good places to train. Choose the one whose daily life you want to wake up to.

**Is this yoga teacher training Yoga Alliance certified?**

No. Santhi is not a Yoga Alliance Registered Yoga School, and we would rather say so plainly than let you find out later. Graduates receive the school’s own 200-hour certificate. **Do I need to be an advanced practitioner?**

No. You do not need to be flexible or able to do advanced postures. What helps most is a steady daily practice for a few weeks before you arrive, so that two practices a day feels demanding but not shocking. [How to prepare](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/preparing-for-yoga-teacher-training/)

**Which month is best for a teacher training in Kerala?**

January and February are dry, warm and the most comfortable. April is the hottest month on the coast. June brings the start of the monsoon: cooler, greener and quieter, with heavy bursts of rain. [Kerala month by month](https://santhiyogaindia.com/blog/best-time-to-visit-kerala-for-yoga/)
